# FACTION DESIGN SHEETS — Sturgia & Nords
**Last updated:** Session 2 — corrected from erroneous Nord/Sturgia conflation

---

## THE FUNDAMENTAL DISTINCTION

These two factions share a northern geography and a history of conflict with each other.
They do NOT share a military identity, cultural identity, or playstyle. A player should
never confuse them on the battlefield.

| | Sturgia | Nords |
|---|---|---|
| **Historical basis** | Kievan Rus / Slavic princes | Norse Vikings / Scandinavians |
| **Core identity** | River lords, forest warriors, settled nobility | Sea raiders, coastal conquerors, warrior culture |
| **Economic base** | River trade, fur, timber, agriculture | Raiding, coastal settlements, tribute |
| **Military core** | Heavy spear & shield infantry, druzhina cavalry | Axe & shield shock infantry, longship raiders |
| **Relationship** | Rivals — Nords raid Sturgian coasts and rivers. Sturgia views Nords as a persistent existential threat. Nords view Sturgia as the richest target within reach. |

## STURGIA — THE RIVER PRINCES

### Historical Inspiration
Kievan Rus, 9th–12th century. A Slavic civilization built on river trade routes —
the great rivers were their highways, their borders, and their lifeblood. Their
princes (druzhina) were warrior-nobles who maintained personal retinues of elite
cavalry and heavy infantry. The common folk were fierce but poorly equipped.
They borrowed heavily from their Byzantine neighbors to the south in arms, religion,
and statecraft — and from the steppe nomads to the east in cavalry tactics.

### Cultural Identity
- River towns, wooden fortifications (gorod), dense northern forests
- A stratified society: princes at the top, free warriors (druzhina), commoners, thralls
- Suspicious of outsiders, deeply loyal to their prince
- The forest is home territory — they know it, they use it, they fight in it
- Bitter historical experience with both Norse raiders AND steppe nomads — they have
  been hammered from multiple directions and survived by being stubborn and adaptable

### Military Philosophy
Sturgia fights a war of attrition and terrain denial. They do not rush you like the Nords.
They hold ground. They use the forest. Their druzhina cavalry is real and dangerous — unlike
the Nords who have almost no cavalry at all. A Sturgian army is a combined-arms force:
heavy infantry anchor, cavalry harassment, archers in forest cover.

### Combat Identity
**"The wall that bites back."**
Sturgian infantry forms a line and holds it. But unlike the Empire's disciplined
formation fighting, Sturgia's line is aggressive — they press forward, they push,
they want close-quarters where their axes and strength matter most.
They are NOT berserkers. They are disciplined heavy infantry who happen to be
very aggressive when they close to melee.

### Unique Mechanic — *Druzhina Bond*
Elite druzhina units gain a stacking morale bonus for every friendly druzhina unit
within close range. A lone druzhina is dangerous. A group of druzhina in close
formation is a different order of threat. Separating them is a valid counter-strategy.

### Unique Mechanic — *Forest Discipline*
Sturgian infantry in forest terrain: +15% melee damage, -10% damage received.
They know the terrain. Fighting them in the woods is fighting them at their best.

### Troop Tree Structure
```
Sturgian Levy (T1)
├── Sturgian Warrior (T2) → Sturgian Veteran (T3) → Sturgian Guard (T4) → Sturgian Bogatyr (T5) → Druzhina Champion (T6)
│                                                 └── Sturgian Spearman (T4) → Sturgian Pike Guard (T5)
├── Sturgian Scout (T2) → Sturgian Raider Cavalry (T3) → Sturgian Druzhina (T4) → Druzhina Lancer (T5)
└── Sturgian Archer (T2) → Sturgian Forest Archer (T3) → Sturgian Veteran Archer (T4)
```

### Equipment Identity
- Chainmail hauberks, lamellar vests over padding, kaftans
- Round shields (not kite — that's Vlandia)
- Axes, spears, one-handed swords (no great swords — that's Nords)
- Fur-lined cloaks, leather boots, conical helmets with mail aventail
- Druzhina: Byzantine-influenced lamellar, decorated helmets, proper cavalry gear
- Color palette: deep greens, dark browns, earth tones — forest colors

### Visual Silhouette
At a glance: round shield, conical or dome helmet with aventail, fur trim. Stocky,
layered look. Druzhina cavalry stands apart — brighter colors, decorated armor.
Should look like they walked out of a Rus illuminated manuscript.

### Faction Lore Hook
Sturgia is a confederation of river-prince city-states loosely unified under a
high prince. Internal politics are constant — princes compete for influence,
river trade rights, and access to the southern passes. The high kingship is
elective among the princes, which means every succession is a crisis. This is
mechanically represented: Sturgia has the highest internal faction tension of
any non-Nord northern faction. Their lords frequently disagree, and the player
can exploit this.

### Rivalry with Nords — Sturgian Perspective
The Nords come every summer. They come up the rivers in their longships and they
take what they can carry. Sturgian settlements near the coast are fortified not
against other Sturgian princes but against Nord raids. Every Sturgian lord
has lost something to a Nord warband. The hatred is personal, generational, and
completely mutual. In diplomatic terms: Sturgia and Nords have a permanent
negative relation floor. Peace between them is always temporary. War is the
default state.

## NORDS — THE SEA KINGS

### Historical Inspiration
Norse Vikings, 8th–11th century. Not just raiders — conquerors, settlers, traders,
and kingdom-builders. Their longships gave them projection power no land-based faction
could match. They could strike anywhere with a coastline or a navigable river. Their
military culture was built around personal valor, the shield wall (skjaldborg), and
the bond between a jarl and his hirdmen.

### Cultural Identity
- The sea is everything — identity, economy, military projection, religion
- A culture of honor, saga, and remembered deeds — reputation matters deeply
- Jarls rule by the loyalty of their hird, not by feudal obligation
- Raiding is not shameful — it is how young warriors prove themselves
- Death in battle is preferable to old age — this has mechanical consequences
- They have settled some coastal areas but remain fundamentally maritime

### Military Philosophy
The Nords hit first, hit hard, and overwhelm before the enemy can organize.
Their entire military doctrine is built on the shock of the initial assault.
They cross the water fast, they land, they form a shield wall, and they advance.
If the enemy holds, it becomes a grinding melee that the Nords are also very
good at. Their weakness: no cavalry to speak of, poor performance in sustained
siege warfare without their ships, and vulnerability to sustained missile fire
if they cannot close the distance.

### Combat Identity
**"The wave that doesn't stop."**
A Nord shield wall advances. It does not hold — it presses. Individual Nords
are exceptionally dangerous in melee: high strength, two-handed weapon mastery,
berserker elite troops who abandon shields entirely for devastating two-handed
axes. Fighting Nords is about stopping their advance before they reach you.
Once they are in your lines, you are in serious trouble.

### Unique Mechanic — *Warsails Projection*
Nords can deploy from coastal tiles that other factions cannot reach. Their
raid parties can appear from the sea, raid coastal settlements, and withdraw
before a land army can respond. This is a strategic-layer mechanic: Nords
are a constant coastal pressure that land-bound factions cannot fully pin down.

### Unique Mechanic — *Skjaldborg Advance*
When Nord infantry are in shield wall formation and advancing (moving forward
into the enemy), they receive a stacking damage resistance bonus that grows
as they close the distance. They are hardest to stop at the moment of impact.
Standing and shooting at an advancing Nord shield wall works — but you need
to break it before they hit you. Once contact is made, the bonus fades.

### Unique Mechanic — *Hird Valor*
Nord hirdmen (elite household warriors) gain morale from killing, not from
winning. Each kill by a hirdman unit generates a small morale pulse. Their
morale can sustain longer than their numbers suggest — they fight harder
the bloodier the battle gets.

### Troop Tree Structure
```
Nord Thrall (T1)  ← conscripted, poorly equipped, not truly Nord
├── Nord Raider (T2) → Nord Warrior (T3) → Nord Huscarl (T4) → Nord Hirdman (T5) → Jarl's Champion (T6)
│                                        └── Nord Berserker (T4) → Ulfhednar (T5)  ← no shield, dual-axe, glass cannon
└── Nord Skirmisher (T2) → Nord Skald Archer (T3) → Nord Veteran Archer (T4)
```
*Note: No cavalry branch. This is intentional. Nords do not field meaningful cavalry.*
*Their power projection comes from the sea, not from mounted units.*

### Equipment Identity
- Round shields (large, reinforced — proper Viking shields, heavier than Sturgian)
- Two-handed axes, one-handed axes, one-handed swords
- Chainmail, some lamellar for elites, minimal plate
- Winged or horned helmets only for lore — actual combat helmets are round with
  nasal guard or spectacle guard (historically accurate)
- Fur cloaks, wool tunics, leather bracers
- Berserkers: minimal armor by design — bare chest or light padding, two axes
- Color palette: greys, deep blues, bone white, iron — sea colors

### Visual Silhouette
At a glance: large round shield, simple round helmet, axe. Berserkers stand out
immediately — no shield, bare, two-handed axe, terrifying. Hirdmen are visually
distinguished by better chainmail, decorated helmets, and quality swords.
Should look like they stepped off a longship.

### Faction Lore Hook
The Nords are not a unified kingdom — they are a collection of jarls who follow
a high jarl (the Sea King) when it serves them. The Sea King's authority comes
from victory and tribute. A Sea King who stops winning loses followers fast.
This is mechanically represented: Nord faction cohesion is directly tied to
their recent military success. A losing Nord faction fragments faster than any
other. A winning Nord faction snowballs aggressively as smaller jarls flock to
a successful Sea King.

### Rivalry with Sturgia — Nord Perspective
The Sturgians have the richest river towns and the most silver. Raiding them
is tradition and profit. Some Nords have settled among the Sturgians, which
the pure-blooded Sturgian princes resent. The Nords do not view the conflict
as a war — they view it as the natural order. Strong takes from weak.
The fact that Sturgia keeps fighting back is, frankly, the only reason the
Nords respect them at all.

## THE STURGIA-NORD RIVALRY — MECHANICAL IMPLEMENTATION

### Permanent Relation Floor
Sturgia and Nords have a hardcoded minimum relation of -50. No amount of
diplomacy can bring them to true peace. Truces are possible. Alliance is not.

### Coastal Raid Events
Nords generate seasonal raid events against Sturgian coastal and river settlements.
These are not player-triggered — they happen on AI schedule. The player ruling
Sturgia must manage this constant pressure. The player ruling Nords can direct
these raids for strategic gain.

### Contested Settlements
Two or three settlements on the Sturgian coast are flagged as historically
contested — they have changed hands between Sturgia and Nords within living
memory. These settlements have split populations, lower loyalty floors, and
generate inter-faction tension events regardless of who currently holds them.

### Cultural Crossover Units
A small number of mixed-heritage units exist — Nord settlers who have adopted
Sturgian ways, or Sturgian warriors who have sailed with Nord warbands.
These are rare, recruitable only under specific conditions, and serve as
a narrative bridge between the two factions without blurring their identities.
