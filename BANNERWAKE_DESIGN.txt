SECTION 1: CORE PHILOSOPHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Bannerwake respects what Bannerlord is, but is not afraid to rebuild what it
should have been. Where vanilla falls short of its own vision, Bannerwake
finishes the thought. This is a private standalone overhaul, not a compatibility
mod. It is for personal use only, drawing assets from other mods privately.

Campaign mode assumption: NO DEATH, NO BIRTH. No mechanics depend on killing
heirs or waiting for births. Never implement anything that does.

Use it as hard canon for vanilla/War Sails lord IDs, clans, kingdoms, ages,

Dialogue is persona/tag/state driven, not a bespoke transcript attached to
each lord. Bannerwake-authored lord dialogue may be written from XML voice
labels and traits, but sample lines are design writing unless a specific
vanilla string ID is cited.

LOCAL ENGINE FEASIBILITY LOCK — 2026-06-05:
Target install is Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord beta v1.4.5 with official
NavalDLC / War Sails v1.2.5 loaded. The local NavalDLC SubModule.xml requires
base v1.4.5 and loads campaign XML for ships, ship hulls, ship slots, ship
upgrade pieces, naval party templates, naval cultures, kingdoms, clans, heroes,
settlements, ports, shipyards, naval equipment, naval lords, naval characters,
and XSLT transforms. Bannerwake must extend these local systems instead of
recreating them blindly.

Code-facing implementation boundaries:
- XML can define cultures, clans/factions, kingdoms, heroes, NPCCharacters,
  party templates, items, equipment rosters, settlements, ship data, banners,
  text, and scenes. XML alone cannot run Bannerwake's dynamic state machine,
  karma, event cards, custom AI vows, land seizures, port blockades, economic
  timers, Ghilman contract voids, Golden Boar memory, Kosmos fragments, or
  Vrak/Black Keel re-emergence.
- Campaign logic belongs in C# CampaignBehaviorBase classes with saved state
  through SyncData. Use CampaignEvents such as DailyTick, HourlyTick,
  DailyTickClan, DailyTickParty, DailyTickSettlement, OnSettlementEntered,
  Before/AfterSettlementEntered, OnSettlementOwnerChanged, OnPlayerBattleEnd,
  OnClanChangedKingdom, WarDeclared/MakePeace, OnBlockadeActivated/
  OnBlockadeDeactivated, OnShipCreated/Destroyed/OwnerChanged/Repaired, and
  mobile-party naval navigation/raft state events where applicable.
- Do not write fake API names as if they are confirmed. If an event is desired
  but not locally confirmed, describe it as custom polling or a Bannerwake
  wrapper, not a vanilla hook.
- Settlement ownership changes must use TaleWorlds action APIs such as
  ChangeOwnerOfSettlementAction.ApplyByDefault/ApplyBySiege/ApplyByRebellion
  or a deliberately chosen adjacent ApplyBy... method. Do not directly mutate
  owner fields in gameplay code. Kingdom creation/defection/mercenary status
  must use ChangeKingdomAction methods.
- War/peace state must use DeclareWarAction and MakePeaceAction or kingdom
  decision paths, not silent relation edits.
- Party AI promises require DefaultMobilePartyAIModel replacement, Harmony
  patches, or faction-specific behavior gates. XML party templates can shape
  parties, but they cannot enforce "never raids villages," "never breaks a
  contract," "avoids open battle," or "voids contract before Ghilman-vs-Ghilman."
- Wages and recruitment cost are controlled through the wage/recruitment model
  surface, locally visible as DefaultPartyWageModel methods such as
  GetCharacterWage and GetTotalWage. Do not invent or reference a fake
  troop-wage API method name.
- Weather/naval danger should build on DefaultMapWeatherModel,
  DefaultCampaignShipDamageModel, ShipHelper, PortStateHelper,
  DefaultSettlementPatrolModel, ship/port XML, and NavalDLC blockade/ship
  events. Storms, winter-sea asymmetry, port restrictions, and ship damage are
  feasible, but they are not automatic faction behavior without code.
- Local minor factions are Faction records with is_minor_faction="true" and,
  usually, four minor_faction_character_templates. The old "hard cap of three
  named heroes" claim is false as an engine statement. Three captains may be a
  design budget, not a local engine limit.
- Ordinary vanilla minor-faction loops are not built for permanent settlement
  ownership. A sub-faction that owns land long-term must be promoted to a
  pseudo-kingdom/full kingdom structure or receive equivalent C# handling for
  ownership, garrisons, clans, parties, diplomacy, save/load, AI protection,
  encyclopedia state, and recovery.
- Campaign mode assumes NO DEATH and NO BIRTH as a Bannerwake design dependency.
  Local APIs include birth/death events, but Bannerwake must not depend on them.
- Armor color is conditional. Many vanilla body armor items support
  UseTeamColor; NavalDLC items are mixed, with many items lacking the flag.
  Use native dynamic color sections where supported. Otherwise create bw_
  material/texture variants and normalize texture/shading quality manually.
- "Fire" is partly supported by vanilla/War Sails surfaces such as fire pots,
  fireships, fire ballista, ship fire damage, and OnShipBurned-style mission
  events. Undying Flame infantry fire arrows, burning thrown weapons, and
  burning melee weapons remain custom mission/combat behavior until proven by
  source code.
- Battania's Ca fál, yew-branch sacrifice, and Dunglanys grove layer is
  Bannerwake design canon unless a vanilla string ID is cited. Local XML search
  did not confirm those names/phrases as literal vanilla strings.
- External mods in the reference library are research targets only unless their
  source code has been read against v1.4.5/War Sails. Mod descriptions, Nexus
  pages, and Steam descriptions are not implementation proof.

THE EIGHT PILLARS:

1. RESPECT AND REBUILD
   Vanilla players should still recognize Calradia. But everything must feel
   more intentional, more alive, more dangerous.

2. VISUAL IDENTITY FIRST
   Troops must look like they belong to their culture, role, and tier. Silhouette
   must communicate who they are and how dangerous they are. Armor chosen by
   visual fit, not item ID.

3. LORE AS MECHANICS
   Faction history, beliefs, rivalries, traditions must affect troops, battles,
   events, recruitment, diplomacy, economy, and campaign behavior. Lore is not
   flavor text. It is the design brief.

4. COHERENT DIFFICULTY
   Enemies are dangerous because they use their strengths — equipment, terrain,
   morale, formation, party composition. Not inflated stats.

5. NO ASSET DUMPING
   Every armor, weapon, unit, event, and feature must serve the world. Nothing
   added because it is available. Everything earns its place.

6. WAR SAILS MATTER
   The sea is a real strategic layer. Dangerous coasts, pirates, raiders, ports,
   fleets, marine troops, coastal politics, threats from the water. Players who
   ignore the sea suffer for it.

7. EARNED PROGRESSION
   Elite troops, rare equipment, powerful allies, meaningful victories must feel
   like achievements. Nothing trivially available.

8. GROUNDED TONE
   Historically inspired and immersive. Low-fantasy elements only as rare myths,
   relics, elite traditions, or late-game threats. The world is grounded. Danger
   can feel unnatural. The explanation never comes easily.

EMOTIONAL TARGET — the player's first session should contain:
- Troops that look like real soldiers with a culture behind them
- An enemy party that makes them hesitate (they don't know what it is)
- A faction event that feels reactive, not random
- A coastal approach that makes them realize the sea is a threat, not scenery

FANTASY CALIBRATION:
- No magic systems. No glowing weapons as baseline. No fantasy races.
- Relics and myths exist. Rare, hard to find, power always ambiguous.
- Late-game threats escalate into something genuinely terrifying — coherent,
  lore-grounded, in the Prophesy of Pendor tradition.
- The wrongness of Black Keel is the ceiling. Nothing more overt than that.

SECTION 3: MAJOR FACTION IDENTITIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STURGIA — Grand Principality of the North
  Cultural inspiration: Kievan Rus / pure Slavic. Grand Prince Raganvad.

  Soul: Sturgia is a principality held together by tradition and managed tension.
    Raganvad is not a weak ruler — he is a ruler whose strength has been spent
    maintaining something that should not require this much maintenance. The
    boyars have their own armies, their own agendas, their own ideas about what
    Sturgia should be. He governs by balancing their ambitions against each other,
    spending authority carefully. This works. It is also exhausting. A Sturgian
    war that goes badly doesn't merely lose territory — it empowers the boyars
    who questioned the decision. His precarious position is the permanent
    condition of Sturgian kingship, not a temporary weakness.

  What it FEELS like: A Sturgian army is patient. It builds its shieldwall and
    waits. When you attack, the wall absorbs. It doesn't break — it grinds.
    Fighting Sturgia feels like running into stone. Breaking through requires
    something, and by the time you've used it, they have more stone.

  Visual: Sturgia XML colors FF1C2A50 / FF949CCC on armor dynamic sections,
    shields, cloth, and banners. Iron grey, fur trim, high-tier brocade as
    secondary materials. Round shields with Slavic knotwork — spiral and
    interlace patterns, no Nordic rune or geometric influence. Peaked gilded
    helmets at high tier.

  Military: Shieldwall infantry core. High morale, holds when others rout.
    Cavalry on flanks — not the arm that decides, the arm that preserves.
    Wins through endurance. Loses when the enemy has longer endurance or
    destroys the flanks before the shieldwall grinds them down.

  Naval: The northern lake and river highway. Sturgian river ships — flat-
    bottomed, oar-driven, built for lake and river trade — are not raiding
    vessels. They are supply arteries. Every Sturgian river settlement depends
    on them. Cut Sturgian river access and you cut their supply chain more
    effectively than winning three field battles. They know this. Protecting
    river access is a Sturgian strategic obsession.

  Internal politics: The Boyar Council is Sturgia's structural vulnerability.
    Named boyars have personal armies, personal grievances, personal ambitions.
    Raganvad governs by managing their ambitions against each other. Too much
    military failure tips the balance from management to crisis.

  Lore mechanic: BOYAR COUNCIL [designed]:
    Trigger: Sturgia loses 3+ settlements in a single war OR their capital city
      is threatened. Tracked by C# settlement-count variable.
    Event fires: Named boyars are called to council. Each has a position:
      Unified Warpath (the confident boyar who believes he knows better than
        Raganvad about military targets), Internal Independence (the boyar who
        sees Raganvad's weakness and considers succession alternatives),
        Diplomatic Fracture (the pragmatist who wants to negotiate before
        losing more).
    Player role: If present in a Sturgian city, the player receives an
      invitation. Past interactions with each boyar shape available options.
      Attendance is optional. Absence means decisions are made without you.
    Outcomes:
      Unified Warpath wins: Sturgia mobilizes aggressively. Raganvad retains
        authority. Next major campaign is more coordinated.
      Internal Independence wins: A boyar moves toward semi-independence.
        Sturgian military effectiveness drops. May open a Karakhergit-parallel
        fracture path if sustained.
      Diplomatic Fracture wins: Raganvad negotiates a peace. Buys time. The
        underlying tension remains.
      Player actively backs Raganvad's position: He remembers. It costs him
        political capital with the dissenting boyars. He spends it. Whether
        this was the right call depends on what comes next.


VLANDIA — The Kingdom of the Lion
  Cultural inspiration: Norman feudal Europe. King Derthert dey Meroc.

  Soul: Vlandia is a kingdom that looks like strength and is quietly losing its
    coherence. The heraldry is immaculate, the cavalry is the finest in Calradia,
    the king's court has more formality than anyone needs. Under it all, four
    barons pulling in opposite directions have created permanent managed dissent,
    a criminal king holds five of their best settlements, and the actual king is
    a decent man who cannot make anyone afraid of him. Vlandia should be the
    dominant faction. It is not, because everyone in it is too busy watching
    everyone else.

  What it FEELS like: Meeting a Vlandian lord is meeting a performance. Every
    word is weighed, every gesture calculated. The king himself is the exception
    — Derthert asks real questions and waits for real answers, which is why the
    barons consistently underestimate him. On the battlefield, a Vlandian army
    is a mechanism: each element designed to enable the others. Disrupting any
    one piece disrupts all three.

  Visual: Vlandia XML colors FF5C2017 / FFECBA44 on armor dynamic sections,
    shields, cloth, and banners. Kite and heater shields with heraldic lion.
    Heavy plate at elite tier. Every unit looks like it belongs to someone's
    banner — because it does.

  Military: Heavy cavalry charge with crossbow suppression. Crossbows create
    the conditions; cavalry exploits them. Infantry holds the flanks while this
    plays out. Disrupting the coordination is the only way to beat them — the
    parts individually are each good; the combination is exceptional.

  Naval: Amphibious invasion specialists. Vlandian galleys carry knights.
    A Vlandian naval landing does not scatter to loot — it forms and marches.
    The difference between a Vlandian coastal landing and a Nord raid is the
    difference between an invasion and a robbery. The Renegati operate Vlandia's
    southern sea lanes. Vlandia's relationship with naval trade is therefore
    complicated.

  Internal politics: Full political design in Section 12a. The Ingalther-Aldric-
    Calatild triangle, Vrak's destabilization, Derthert's precarious position,
    Ferrand's intelligence network, Cult of Kosmos infiltration — all authored.

  Lore mechanic: FEUDAL OBLIGATION CRISIS [designed]:
    Trigger: Derthert loses any major siege (own clan's settlement OR the
      capital city Pravend). Tracked by specific battle-outcome flag in C#.
    Event fires: Parchment card. Lord loyalty scores for low-loyalty barons
      drop. Player notified of "rumblings at court."
    Player role: If in Pravend within 5 days of the siege loss, can attend an
      informal council. Correct responses (based on relationship and karma axes)
      can stabilize the most affected lords.
    Outcomes:
      Without player intervention: 1-2 barons begin operating more
        independently. Campaign decisions less coordinated.
      With player stabilization: Crisis deferred. Derthert creates conditions
        — a more favorable contract, a piece of intelligence the player needed.
        He doesn't thank directly. The world shifts slightly.
      Long-term instability (crisis fires 3+ times): A formal defection event
        fires. A baron leaves Derthert's service. This cannot be undone without
        significant player intervention.


THE EMPIRE — Three Claimants, One Legacy
  Cultural inspiration: Late Roman / early Byzantine. Three heirs to a shattered
  civilization. Northern: Lucon, Southern: Rhagaea, Western: Garios.

  Soul: The Empire's tragedy is being right about everything while still losing.
    The Imperial administrative model — bureaucracy, formation warfare, supply
    chain discipline, cultural assimilation — is objectively the most effective
    governing system in Calradia. None of this helps when the empire is three
    factions instead of one and every advantage requires unified direction. Each
    claimant believes their legitimacy is real. Each is correct in a different
    way. Each is wrong in a different way. The tragedy is genuine.

  What it FEELS like: An Imperial army is uncomfortable to fight. It does not
    make obvious mistakes. It doesn't break when you expect it to, doesn't
    pursue when pursuit would be dangerous, doesn't commit cavalry until the
    moment is exactly right. Beating an Empire army once feels like you got
    away with something. A second battle with the same general feels completely
    different — they remembered what you did and they prepared for it.

  Visual: Each Empire faction uses its local XML colors on dynamic armor sections,
    shields, cloth, and banners; silver scale, crested helmets, and eagle shields
    are the shared Imperial material language.
    Northern:  FF39223F / FFDE9953 — slate and gold, the most austere
    Southern:  FF9382D0 / FFDE9953 — lighter purple, more ornate, the richest
    Western:   FF9E5072 / FFDE9953 — dark burgundy, the most embattled

  Military: Formation warfare. Infantry holds. Archers punish movement.
    Cavalry exploits gaps at exactly the right moment. The Empire wins by not
    making mistakes and by having the supply chain to outlast every other faction
    in a long campaign. The most disciplined and versatile army in Calradia.
    Losing to three smaller factions because discipline requires unified command.

  Naval: Harbor control and supply chain warfare. The Empire wins naval
    engagements by denying ports and maintaining supply lines, not by sinking
    ships. Not glamorous. Devastatingly effective in long campaigns.

  Internal politics: Three legitimate claimants. Each believes their claim is
    correct. Each is wrong in a different way.

  Lore mechanic: IMPERIAL LEGITIMACY [designed]:
    Trigger: One Empire faction controls 60%+ of former Imperial territory.
      Tracked by settlement-count variable per faction.
    Event fires: World-wide parchment card. The controlling faction formally
      claims the title of Restored Empire. All other Imperial factions and
      eligible players have a response window.
    Response options:
      Contest the claim (military): Other factions declare joint war on the
        claimant. Temporary military alliance between rivals.
      Recognize the claim: Massive political concession. The recognizing
        faction subordinates or merges. World-altering consequence.
      Counter-claim: A second faction asserts their own legitimacy.
        Generates a double legitimacy crisis — two "Restored Empires," neither
        recognized by the other. Conflict follows. Everything accelerates.
      Player vote: If player is a senior vassal of any Empire faction,
        their faction's response is influenced by player preference and
        existing relationship quality.


BATTANIA — The Old Kingdoms
  Cultural inspiration: Pictish, Irish, Welsh Celtic. King Caladog.

  Soul: Battania lives by older rules than anyone else in Calradia, and those
    rules are the source of both its strength and its absolute limits. The Ca fál
    gives the Battanians something no other faction has: a moral framework that
    the land itself enforces. Groves that cannot be burned without consequence.
    Holy men who travel freely through enemy territory by ancient right. Gatherings
    that respond to desecration with overwhelming force. None of this translates
    to conventional military-political power. All of it shapes everything.

  What it FEELS like: You do not find Battanian armies. They find you. They
    choose the ground, choose the moment, and attack from directions you didn't
    know existed. On open plains they are dangerous. In forests they are something
    else entirely — the trees start moving and then it's over.

  Religion: Ca fál — Bannerwake-authored Battanian religious layer. Two primary
    gods, sacred groves, Holy Men who travel freely by ancient law, ancestor-
    speaking through sacred lake water, yew-branch sacrifice at the grove outside
    Dunglanys. Not XML-confirmed vanilla strings. Bannerwake design canon.

  Visual: Battania XML colors FF2D3F1D / FFBFCBB0 on armor dynamic sections,
    shields, cloth, and banners. Grey linen and dark iron as secondary materials.
    Celtic knotwork on shields and cloaks. Woad at T1-T2 only — the regular
    Battanian army is disciplined and armored, not a rabble. Lean silhouettes.
    A Battanian does not look armored because they don't want to be seen.
    From T3 upward: armored, proud, culturally distinct.

  Military: Ambush and terrain exploitation. Lethal in forests and broken ground.
    Vulnerable on open plains. All troops carry throwing weapons — javelins or
    throwing axes — used in the moment before melee contact. A short-range volley
    followed by immediate melee charge. The effect is devastating in the right
    terrain and predictable in the wrong terrain. Desired AI behavior: avoid
    pitched battle on open ground unless advantaged. Requires MobilePartyAI/
    Harmony behavior work, not XML-only troop design.

  Naval: Rivers and estuaries. Battanian boats are small, fast, go where heavier
    ships cannot. They appear from river mouths, strike specific targets, vanish
    upstream. Not a naval power. A coastal threat that becomes significant if
    ignored long enough.

  Internal politics: Caladog holds Battania through the Ca fál's political
    weight more than through military dominance. The Grove Council must approve
    major wars. Slow to commit. Unbreakable once committed.

  Lore mechanic: GROVE COUNCIL [designed]:
    Trigger: Battania attempts to declare war on any major faction.
    Event fires: The Grove Council convenes. Named clan chiefs present their
      positions. The player, if in Battanian territory with standing, may attend.
    Duration: 5-7 in-game days of deliberation. The war cannot be declared
      during this window. Enemies can prepare. This is the price of the old law.
    Outcomes:
      Council approves unanimously: Battania declares war with every clan
        committed. Their military effectiveness is higher than usual — the wall
        that chose to fight is different from one that was ordered to.
      Council declines: Caladog cannot override the Grove Council. War does
        not happen. A player lobbying for it must find another path.
      Split decision: Some clans committed, some abstained. Partial military
        support. Uncommitted lords do not appear in Battanian army compositions
        for that war. Their absence is felt in every pitched battle.


ASERAI — The Sultanate of the Desert
  Cultural inspiration: Arab caliphate and Bedouin tradition. Sultan Unqid.

  Soul: The Aserai are among the oldest surviving civilizations in Calradia and
    carry that history like armor. Their confidence is not arrogance — it is the
    settled certainty of a culture that has outlasted everything thrown at it.
    The desert is not an obstacle for the Aserai. It is their home and their
    primary weapon. Armies that enter the sultanate without understanding the
    desert don't fight their way out. They run out of water and make mistakes
    and eventually stop being armies.

  What it FEELS like: Aserai cavalry is the most elegantly dangerous force in
    Calradia. Composite archers thin your formation while you're still too far
    to respond; Faris cavalry waits until you're committed and can't easily
    retreat; Mameluke lancers arrive when the decision has already been made.
    There are no wasted movements. Everything was planned before your scouts
    reported your position.

  Visual: Aserai XML colors FF965228 / FF4F2212 on armor dynamic sections,
    shields, cloth, and banners. White linen over mail and brass metals as
    secondary materials. Turbaned helmets throughout. Round calligraphic shields.
    Not light desert skirmishers — a proud sultanate military. The wallpaper
    shows what they should look like at T3-T4. Use it.

  Military: Mounted harassment into decisive cavalry charge. Composite archers
    thin formations, Faris cavalry exploits the thinning, Mameluke lancers
    finish. Poor in sieges, exceptional in open mobile warfare. The desert
    terrain multiplies every advantage.

  Naval: The Aserai are the southern sea's masters by tradition and geography.
    Dhow fleets control trade routes. Every southern port has an Aserai merchant
    presence that shades into an Aserai intelligence presence. Their navy is
    commercial and threatening simultaneously — trade partners who could become
    something else if the relationship changes.

  Internal politics: Sultan Unqid is unchallenged and competent. The weakness
    is economic, not political. The Caravan Economy is the structural
    vulnerability. The Beni Zilal's legitimacy challenge only becomes real
    when the economic disruption creates political instability.

  Lore mechanic: CARAVAN DISRUPTION [designed]:
    Trigger: Player or enemy systematically destroys Aserai caravans. C#
      caravan-destruction tracker counts losses over a rolling 30-day window.
    Phases:
      Economic pressure (10+ caravans destroyed): Aserai party wages become
        harder to maintain. Recruitment from certain towns drops. Small
        prosperity penalties to urban settlements.
      Military strain (20+ caravans): Aserai armies spawn at reduced size.
        Wage pressure fires internal events — soldiers not paid on time.
        The Beni Zilal stir if already ACTIVE.
      Crisis (30+ caravans in 30 days): Formal event fires. The Sultan
        declares trade routes a military priority. Dedicated patrol armies
        assembled. If a player caused the disruption, those patrols have
        specific intelligence about the player.
    Recovery: Caravans rebuild if disruption stops for 30 days. Sustained
      disruption is much harder to recover from than a short burst.


KHUZAIT — The Steppe Khanate
  Cultural inspiration: Mongol and central Asian steppe tradition. Monchug Khan.

  Soul: The Khuzait are the most dangerous cavalry force in Calradia and cannot
    hold a city without making everyone in it miserable, including themselves.
    This is not a limitation — it is the entire dynamic. Monchug Khan unified
    the clans through military genius that has no peacetime application. On the
    steppe, the Khuzait are sovereign. Everywhere else they are an occupying
    force waiting for someone to make holding the territory more expensive than
    leaving it.

  What it FEELS like: A Khuzait army is not an army. It is weather. It appears
    from multiple directions before you're oriented, surrounds you while you're
    still deciding what direction to face, and vanishes the moment you commit to
    pursuit. If you stay still, it circles until your supply runs out. If you
    advance, it retreats faster than you can follow. The only way to fight Khuzait
    is to be somewhere they absolutely must be, force them to commit, and win
    fast before they recalculate. They know you know this. They account for it.

  Visual: Khuzait XML colors FF468C7C / FFCCBB89 on armor dynamic sections,
    shields, cloth, and banners. Lamellar with steppe aesthetic and horse-hair
    plumes. Small round shields. On a ship, Khuzait troops look wrong.
    Intentional. They belong on horses.

  Military: Swarm, feign retreat, encircle. Cannot be caught or pinned. Weak in
    sustained sieges and narrow terrain. Every Khuzait infantry unit is a cavalry
    warrior who dismounted — carrying the same aesthetics, the same mobility
    mindset, in a context that punishes both.

  Naval: NONE. River boats and ferry crossings only. Khuzait cannot threaten
    by sea. This strategic fact must be felt by the player as a permanent
    constraint — a faction that controls vast territory cannot project that
    power across water.

  Internal politics: Khan's Unity. Monchug Khan holds the clans together through
    personal military authority that has no civilian equivalent. Military failure
    gives clan chiefs room to question the arrangement. Karakhergit is the most
    active fracture line. It will not be the last.

  Lore mechanic: KHAN'S UNITY [designed]:
    Trigger: Khuzait loses 4+ settlements to any enemy. Tracked by C# variable.
    Event fires: A clan chief publicly questions the strategic direction.
      World event parchment card fires. Player notified.
    Phases:
      Single challenge (4-6 settlements lost): Monchug addresses it.
        The challenger is brought back into line by demonstration of competence.
        Smoothed over. Tension remains.
      Multiple challenges (7-9 settlements lost): A formal challenge to Khan's
        authority fires. Player present in Khuzait territory can witness or
        participate. The outcome shapes Khuzait's political stability for 90 days.
      Collapse (10+ settlements lost): Karakhergit formally declares their
        ancestral steppe range. One clan chief announces independence. Khan's
        Unity becomes Khan's Crisis. Multiple factions become available to
        exploit the political vacuum.


NORDS — Kingdom of Nordvyg (War Sails DLC)
  Cultural inspiration: Norse/Scandinavian. King Halthdar the Golden.
  Unified a generation ago by his father Volbjorn the Hungry.

  Soul: The Nords are the newest major faction and the most confidently
    assertive. Halthdar's father Volbjorn built Nordvyg from twenty petty
    kingdoms through twenty years of war, gold, and relentless pressure.
    The current generation inherited something their fathers bled for and
    don't fully understand what it cost. Halthdar governs well — better at
    peace than his father, better at diplomacy than any previous Nord king.
    The Ironjarls are counting every moment this isn't quite enough.

  What it FEELS like: A Nord engagement starts with throwing axes at close
    range, transitions immediately to melee, and ends when one side has no one
    standing. There is no cavalry, no maneuver, no organized retreat. The shield
    wall advances. It hits. If it doesn't break you immediately, it reforms and
    hits again. You can win the second engagement. The fourth is harder than the
    second by exactly the amount that surprises you.

  Visual: Nord kingdom colors FF202931 / FFb7623c on armor dynamic sections,
    shields, cloth, banners, and cloaks. Round shields with geometric patterns.
    Axes everywhere. Fur cloaks. Their armor was made by people who live in
    weather that would end a Vlandian campaign in November.

  Military: Pure infantry — NO cavalry. The sea is their mobility. Every Nord
    unit is a mariner by default. No separate mariner line needed. All units
    fight on ships with full effectiveness. Aggressive forward infantry. Shield
    wall that MOVES, not holds. They do not defend — they press.

  Naval: Complete dominance in northern waters. Nord longships are faster,
    lighter, and more maneuverable than anything else on the northern sea.
    Their crews are comfortable in weather that grounds other factions. Nords
    don't have a navy — they ARE a navy. Their naval skill is so embedded it
    is not a specialization; it is the baseline assumption of Nord military
    thought.

  Internal politics: The Ironjarls represent the permanently available threat
    of Nordvyg's political fracture. Nine petty kingdoms, twenty years ago.
    Some bent the knee completely. Some are still remembering. The relationship
    between Halthdar, the Ironjarls, and the Jomsvikingr creates Nordvyg's
    political texture — three distinct Nord power centers with different and
    occasionally contradictory relationships to the unified kingdom. Full detail
    in Section 12b.

  Lore mechanic: THE THING [designed]:
    The seasonal Nord political assembly. Fires in spring and autumn each campaign
    year — two sessions per year, four sessions per typical campaign.
    Each session has:
      A primary agenda set by world state — war preparation, trade dispute,
        clan conflict, Black Keel response, Ironjarl activity, Jomsvikingr
        status change. The agenda reflects the current most pressing Nord
        political issue. It is not predetermined.
      3-5 named speakers drawn from active clan leaders most affected by the
        agenda item. Who speaks tells the player something about the faction's
        current political balance.
      Player attendance option: if in a Nord settlement within range, the player
        receives notification. Attendance is optional. Absence has consequences.
      Speaking rights: depend on relationship with Halthdar and relevant clan
        leaders. A player with high Nord standing can speak. Low standing: observe.
      An outcome fires regardless of player presence — but presence and speaking
        shift the probability distribution of outcomes.
    Missing The Thing: decisions were made without you. Attending means you can
      influence them, not control them. Halthdar makes final calls. His calls
      reflect what he heard. Your presence is a variable in that calculation.

ENGINE CONSTRAINTS (confirmed by local v1.4.5 file audit):
- Minor factions are local Faction records with is_minor_faction="true"; most
  vanilla minor factions also use minor_faction_character_templates rather than
  persistent dynastic Hero rosters. The local SandBox minor factions generally
  define four leader templates, so the old "3 named hero leader cap" is not a
  confirmed engine rule.
- Ordinary minor factions are not designed as permanent landholding kingdoms.
  Settlement ownership can be changed by C# action APIs, but long-term land
  ownership requires pseudo-kingdom/full-kingdom support or equivalent custom
  handling.
- Giving a sub-faction a settlement requires C# using
  ChangeOwnerOfSettlementAction.ApplyByDefault/ApplyBySiege/ApplyByRebellion
  or another deliberately chosen action path, followed by garrison setup,
  home-settlement checks, party/AI handling, diplomacy, save-state, and
  recapture/re-emergence logic.
- Black Keel and Vrak exceed ordinary minor-faction structure deliberately by
  being pseudo-kingdom/custom-kingdom structures made of multiple clans, not a
  single ordinary minor faction.
- Faction XML requires initial_home_settlement for reliable minor-faction
  behavior. The Diathma drift warning is treated as a known modding failure
  mode caused by missing/invalid home settlement data or runtime relocation
  logic, not as a universally reproduced local proof. All new factions need
  explicit home settlement assignment in XML and C# validation on game load.
- All dynamic state transitions need custom C# campaign tracking variables

SUB-FACTION XML NAME LOCK (confirmed against local v1.4.5 + NavalDLC XML):
- Lake Rats: vanilla minor faction id `lakepike`, display/short name Lake Rats.
  Do not use `lake_rats` unless creating a new Bannerwake `bw_` record.
- Jomsvikingr: Bannerwake current display/design name for the existing
  `skolderbrotva` / Skolderbroda mercenary hook. Do not use Skolvard. Either
  migrate `skolderbrotva` deliberately or create explicit `bw_jomsvikingr`
  records; do not pretend `jomsvikingr` already exists in local XML.
- Black Keel: Bannerwake custom/pseudo-kingdom threat. No local XML minor
  faction named Black Keel exists. NavalDLC has `northern_pirates` and
  `southern_pirates`; those are source hooks, not Black Keel itself.
- Company of the Golden Boar: vanilla minor faction id `company_of_the_boar`,
  display name Company of the Golden Boar. Baldric is Bannerwake-invented.
- Grovebound: Bannerwake replacement/supersession of the vanilla `wolfskins`
  idea. No local XML clan/faction named Grovebound exists.
- Hidden Hand: vanilla minor faction id `hidden_hand`.
- Undying Flame: Bannerwake redesign/name for vanilla `embers_of_flame`.
- Last Legion: Bannerwake display/design name for vanilla
  `legion_of_the_betrayed` / Legion of the Betrayed. Local XML text already
  uses the phrase Last Legion, but the faction id/display remains the older
  one unless intentionally migrated.
- Akritoi: Bannerwake frontier-cavalry concept. No local XML minor faction
  named Akritoi/Acritoi exists. Closest XML hook is `eleftheroi`, plus the
  Elaches clan comment "Draw on Akritic ballads." Preserve or migrate this
  deliberately; do not call Akritoi XML-confirmed.
- Jawwal: vanilla minor faction id `jawwal`.
- Ghilman: vanilla minor faction id `ghilman`.
- Beni Zilal: vanilla minor faction id `beni_zilal`.
- Renegati: Bannerwake custom Imperial naval toll faction. No local XML minor
  faction named Renegati exists.
- Ironjarls: Bannerwake Nord political internal faction. No local XML minor
  faction named Ironjarls exists, and they are not mercenaries.

SUB-FACTION STATES: Every sub-faction operates in one of four states:
  DORMANT — minimal activity, barely visible
  ACTIVE  — normal behavior, regular spawning
  ESCALATED — expanded presence, event has fired
  CRISIS  — major world event, player must respond

Transition between states driven by C# campaign variables tracking time,
player inaction, military conditions, and world events. This is the most
complex technical system in Bannerwake. Build as skeleton first, test empty,
then populate with faction behaviors.

--- STURGIAN SUB-FACTIONS ---

LAKE RATS
  Culture: Sturgian (Kievan Rus cultural sphere)
  Leader: The Harbormaster (unnamed, rotates by seniority among senior wreckers)
  Region: Lake Laconis marshes and northern lake shore. NEVER leave the lake.
  Default State: Hostile to all. Wreck ships with false beacons. Rob survivors.
  Escalation: If ignored long enough, Harbormaster consolidates the beacon
    network into a formal toll operation. Any ship on Lake Laconis pays or burns.
    C# coastal-threat event fires after a player-inactivity timer.
  Units: Wrecker (melee, salvage tools as weapons), Beachcomber (light
    skirmisher), Harbormaster's Guard (heavy enforcer, elite tier)
  Threat signature: A ship you didn't know was in trouble. A beacon where no
    lighthouse should be. The wreckage in the morning.
  DREVKO — The Harbormaster:
    No surname. Sturgian underclass — born below the boyar line, found the lake
    more profitable than whatever alternative was available. Has been Harbormaster
    for eleven years, which means he outlasted two predecessors and three attempts
    to replace him through the internal seniority system he himself designed.
    The system that promotes by seniority happened to keep Drevko at the top long
    enough that he became genuinely irreplaceable. Coincidence, he would tell you.
    Personality: Patient, methodical, unexpectedly well-informed. The Lake Rats'
    intelligence network — every boat that works the lake, every merchant who
    pays tribute, every survivor who found staying preferable to leaving — gives
    Drevko a picture of northern Sturgian commerce that rivals Raganvad's own
    intelligence. He sells pieces of this, selectively, to people who prove
    they're worth selling to.
    He does not give speeches. He asks questions. When someone arrives wanting
    to use the Lake Rats for something, Drevko learns more from the hiring
    conversation than from the offer itself. By the time he names a price he
    already knows whether the job will succeed.
    False-beacon mechanic (designed): When ESCALATED, a specific campaign event
    fires — a ship the player knows about is redirected by a false beacon at
    night. The player learns about it through aftermath (missing cargo, missing
    crew, a lamp hung facing the wrong direction on the drifting hull). They can
    investigate. The investigation leads to the beacon network. Destroying the
    network pushes Lake Rats back to ACTIVE. Drevko survives. He rebuilds.
  Notes: KEEP THE NAME EXACTLY. Keep the vibe exactly. Deepen the hierarchy
    and give them the false-beacon mechanic as real gameplay. Accepts fugitives
    from any culture who arrive — they are not ethnically Sturgian only.

STURGIAN SUB-FACTION SLOT 3 — RESERVED
  The third Sturgian slot is deliberately reserved until a concept earns it.
  Sturgia currently has two sub-factions: Lake Rats and The Jomsvikingr.
    Note: Jomsvikingr are Nord by culture but operate in Sturgian-adjacent
    northern coastal territory.
  Do not fill this slot with a forced placeholder.

THE JOMSVIKINGR
  Culture: Nord — THE Jomsviking brotherhood. Not inspired by Jomsvikings.
    They ARE the Jomsvikings. The real thing.
  Leader: Jomshersir Floki. No surname. No epithet needed. Everyone in the
    northern world knows which Floki. He has held the position for twenty years.
    Quiet, calculating, cold. Believes in the code completely AND uses it
    strategically. The most dangerous kind of man.
  Region: Northern coasts. Fixed fortified hall that predates Halthdar's
    unification. Halthdar respects them and leaves them alone. They respect
    Halthdar and ignore him.
  Default State: MERCENARY. Available to any kingdom with gold and reputation.
    The code is ABSOLUTE — no exceptions, ever, even when it destroys someone.
    They never break a contract. Ever. No violence toward women or children.
    No personal feuds within the brotherhood. All loot shared equally.
    Brothers avenged without exception.
  Escalation: If Nordvyg weakens significantly, the Jomsvikingr declare
    independence and fortify their hall as a permanent independent entity.
  THE CODE: Absolute. Not a guideline. A brother shows fear — he is expelled.
    He has been a brother for fifteen years. The code doesn't care. Floki
    enforces it without hesitation. This is what makes them terrifying and
    tragic simultaneously. They chose this. Every morning they choose it again.
    The code creates monsters and heroes simultaneously. Same oath.
  PLAYER ARC (Nord player characters ONLY — the brotherhood is Nord-exclusive):
    The deepest faction arc in Bannerwake. A Nord player can petition to join,
    climb the ranks, and become the finest warrior the brotherhood fields short
    of Floki himself. Never Jomshersir — Floki is always above. The arc runs
    from first contract to Jomsvígr across the entire campaign.
    Entry: Quest chain — Floki sets five specific tasks testing different
      aspects of the code. Fear, retreat, brotherhood, contract, and Floki's
      final question. See Section 11 for full quest and event details.
    Rank system (party size scales with rank AND skills — full detail in
      skill rework session):
      Nýr (20 troops) — just passed the trial, barely recognized
      Jomskarl (60 troops) — recognized Joms-man
      Jomsvakt (120 troops) — guards the brotherhood's interests
      Jomsthegn (200 troops) — Floki's trusted proven warrior
      Jomsstallar (260 troops) — senior officer, Floki's marshal
      Jomsvígr (300 troops) — the finest short of Floki himself
    Exclusive troops: ONLY Jomsvikingr members can recruit Jomsvikingr units.
      Recruited from the Jomsvikingr hall settlement ONLY. No other source.
      No kingdom, player, or lord can field Jomsvikingr troops without
      brotherhood membership.
  TONE REFERENCE: Vinland Saga. Events, dialogue, and encounters should feel
    like that series — the code creating monsters and heroes simultaneously,
    human reality beneath absolute rules, Floki as a calculating force of
    nature rather than a simple warrior-king.
  Threat signature: A shieldwall that holds when everything else has broken.
    The raven banner means someone paid. The raven banner also means Floki
    decided this contract was worth keeping. That distinction matters.
  Notes: Repurposes or supersedes NavalDLC Skolderbroda only after explicit
    migration audit of Faction.skolderbrotva. Raven motif is a Bannerwake
    art-direction lock supported by local NavalDLC assets such as
    `ravenfigurehead`, not an XML-confirmed Jomsvikingr faction-sigil claim.
    The Jomsvikingr's raven is disciplined and oath-bound — distinct from
    Black Keel's corrupted variant.
    Do not use Skolvard as an ID, heading, file name, or code symbol.

UNIVERSAL RULES:
- Every unit at every tier must have a distinguishable silhouette from every
  other unit at the same tier in the same faction
- Armor choices: visual fit FIRST, stat fit second
- No unit wears equipment from another faction's color palette without lore reason
- Each faction must have at least one unit whose silhouette = immediate threat
- Elite units (T5-6) must not spawn in early-game parties
- Branch paths at T4 represent DOCTRINAL CHOICES, not stat upgrades
- Out-of-culture troops cost DOUBLE WAGE for the player clan. Implemented as
  a model/Harmony patch around DefaultPartyWageModel.GetCharacterWage and
  GetTotalWage. Encourages cultural loyalty.
- Non-lootable exclusive armor: certain high-tier units wear armor flagged
  is_merchandise="false" and/or isolated from shops/tournaments/recruit pools.
  Mission pickup/drop behavior must be verified per item type. Use
  CannotBePickedUp-style flags where valid and add custom post-battle loot
  filtering if XML flags do not fully prevent acquisition.

TIER STRUCTURE:
  T1 — Levy/Recruit:    rough cloth, basic tools. They look scared.
  T2 — Militia/Trained: first real weapon, partial armor. Beginning to look
                         like a soldier.
  T3 — Warrior:         coherent armor set. Faction colors appear clearly.
  T4 — Veteran:         full armor. Faction identity unmistakable.
  T5 — Elite:           specialized armor, high-quality weapons. Rare.
                         Immediately threatening.
  T6 — Champion/Guard:  the faction's finest. Named encounter feel.

BRANCH PHILOSOPHY:
  Each branch represents a genuine doctrinal fork, not just different weapons.
  AI lords should also reflect their branch preference in party composition.

MARINE UNITS:
  Every sea-adjacent faction has at least one marine unit type — designed to
  fight on and around ships. Marine units have:
    - Higher Athletics than equivalent land troops
    - Shorter weapons (no polearms that tangle in rigging)
    - May have thrown weapons for boarding actions
    - Available at coastal settlements and port villages, not inland
  Nords and Black Keel have marine variants at nearly every tier.
  Khuzait have ZERO marine units.

GROVEBOUND ARMOR NOTE:
  T4-T5 Grovebound units wear exclusive armor flagged non-droppable/
  non-merchantable where item flags support it, and filtered out of shops,
  tournaments, random loot, and post-battle salvage through C# if needed.
  This is intentional — their armor belongs to the old gods, not the market.

SECTION 6: NAVAL SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The sea is not scenery. Every faction wallpaper in the reference package is set
on or near water. This was not an accident.

THE SEA AS A STRATEGIC LAYER:
- Coastal settlements permanently under threat (scales with Black Keel,
  Sturgian, Renegati activity). Local NavalDLC settlements include port
  coordinates, port scenes, navalraid village scenes, and shipyard buildings.
- Ports are economic multipliers through Bannerwake code. Local XML provides
  port/shipyard data; port income and trade-route health must be computed in
  settlement economy/campaign behavior code.
- Blockading a port is a legitimate war strategy. Local API/audit evidence
  includes blockade activation/deactivation events and PortStateHelper
  restricted/trade states; Bannerwake must connect those to war, trade, patrol,
  and settlement-economy consequences.
- Storm seasons reduce naval activity through weather/ship-damage model work.
  Use DefaultMapWeatherModel, DefaultCampaignShipDamageModel, wind data, and
  ship seaworthiness; do not assume XML alone changes faction movement.
- Nords and Black Keel operate in winter seas that ground Vlandian galleys as
  an authored faction/weather asymmetry. Implement by ship class, seaworthiness,
  party AI, and storm-damage tuning.
- KHUZAIT CANNOT THREATEN BY SEA. This must be felt by the player.

PORT SETTLEMENT MECHANICS:
- Port income: generated by custom settlement-economy behavior based on trade
  route health, local security, blockades, pirate pressure, shipyard level, and
  faction naval control.
- Shipyard building: local NavalDLC settlements already include
  building_shipyard entries. Use or extend those to unlock marine recruitment,
  ship construction/repair/upgrades, and fleet-related quests.
- Coastal fortification: reduces raid damage and adds naval patrol spawns
  through C# tied to settlement buildings and DefaultSettlementPatrolModel.
- Harbormasters: new notable type who gives naval quests and intelligence.
  This requires new notable definitions/dialogue/settlement placement, not just
  a building entry.
- Pirate tribute: some minor settlements pay Renegati or Black Keel —
  player can discover and intervene, or take the arrangement for themselves

THE UNNAMED FLEET (late-game event):
  A fleet with no banner appears on the coastal map. It does not raid immediately.
  It watches. Investigation across multiple quest chains reveals fragments: old
  coins, a language no one speaks, a fighting style no one teaches. Connected to
  Cult of Kosmos. When it strikes, it is coordinated, overwhelming, unlike anything
  encountered before. This is Bannerwake's Pendor event equivalent.
  Never give a complete answer about what the Unnamed Fleet is.

    campaign AI treats this differently from a normal minor faction fight — it

    neutral. Dust Speakers will not take sides. If the steppe becomes a
    battlefield they grow significantly less communicative with everyone involved.

  Hidden Hand vs. Undying Flame:
    When the Undying Flame reaches ESCALATED, the Hidden Hand moves against
    them. Not publicly — the Hand doesn't do public. They begin systematically
    undermining Flame cells: paid informants, diverted tribute, counterfeited
    instructions that send Flame parties to the wrong locations at wrong times.
    The player may discover this operation and be asked to choose a side or
    broker a truce. There is no clean option. The Hand's methods are criminal.
    The Flame's goals are worse.

  Jomsvikingr independence and Nordvyg's response:
    If Nordvyg loses enough territory that the Jomsvikingr independence threshold
    triggers, Halthdar faces an impossible choice: treat them as a breakaway
    faction (politically disastrous — they have done nothing wrong under any
    law), or recognize their independence (creates a precedent the Ironjarls note
    immediately). He does neither. He reduces contact. The Jomsvikingr continue
    operating. The silence between them is loud. Players in both orbits feel
    this tension in every conversation.

  Renegati and Vlandian southern trade:
    Servic dey Valant's arrangement with the Renegati surfaces eventually —
    through player action, world events, or Ferrand's intelligence network.
    When it does: Derthert is publicly furious. The Vlandian council debates it.
    Servic explains his reasoning. He is not entirely wrong — the Renegati were
    going to extract tribute regardless; Servic formalized it and kept the routes
    open. This is a political scandal with a functional justification. Players who
    have interacted with both Servic and the Renegati can speak specifically to
    the arrangement in council. This matters.

  Cult of Kosmos and major faction political destabilization:
    Faces embedded in major factions are not passive observers. They actively
    cultivate the instabilities that serve the arrangement. Vlandia's fracture
    is partly natural; it is partly assisted. Sturgia's boyar tensions are partly
    cultural; they are partly cultivated. The player who has assembled enough
    Kosmos fragments begins to see these patterns. Correlation is not proof.
    Nothing in this arc is ever proof. That is the design.

LATE-GAME THREAT EVENTS:

  THE REAVER TIDE (Black Keel CRISIS):
    Hrothgar the Damned reveals himself. The world notification fires. Black
    Keel seizes Hvalvik via Bannerwake wrapper around ChangeOwnerOfSettlement
    Action. Garrison set to ~2,000 programmatically. Northern coasts enter CRISIS.
    Political consequences cascade through Nordvyg: Orthling clan loses their
    primary settlement, Halthdar cannot ignore it and cannot easily respond,
    Grimulf says nothing publicly and says it very loudly privately.
    Full mechanical and political detail in Sections 9, 9a, and 12b.
    Duration: permanent until Hvalvik is retaken militarily. Recovery resets to
    ESCALATED with timer running. They return.

  THE VESSEL (Undying Flame CRISIS):
    A Flame-converted Imperial town elects the Vessel — living avatar of Emperor
    Darusos. Fourth Empire declared. Fire spreads more aggressively. Party
    composition changes: Flame parties field higher-tier units at greater
    frequency. The Vessel is a unique NPC encounter — meetable once under specific
    conditions. They are sincere. They are not wrong about everything. This is
    the most unsettling thing about them and it should be written that way.

  THE GATHERING (Grovebound CRISIS):
    A Battanian sacred site is desecrated. The Grovebound summon every Ca fál
    adherent. An overwhelming wave descends on the responsible party or settlement
    — then silence. They return to the forest. The Gathering does not claim
    territory. It does not occupy. It responds, punishes, withdraws.
    Players who cause desecrations receive the Gathering as consequence.
    Players who watch an NPC desecrate a sacred site can choose to intervene
    before the threshold is crossed — delaying or preventing the Gathering while
    earning Grovebound relationship. Every choice here is simultaneously moral
    and mechanical.

  CULT OF KOSMOS REVEALED (endgame layer activation):
    Fragments assembled AND Veil Riders encountered. The picture coheres.
    The Unnamed Fleet appears on the coastal map. The game never tells the
    player what to do about it. Five player response options are designed in
    Section 13. The world continues regardless of which the player chooses.
    This is Bannerwake's Prophecy of Pendor equivalent. The difference: there
    is no correct answer, and the mod never tells the player otherwise.

SECTION 9: SUB-FACTION DYNAMIC STATE SYSTEM — FULL SPECIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the highest-risk technical system in Bannerwake. Build it as a skeleton
first. Test it empty. Then populate with faction behaviors one at a time.
Every other system in Bannerwake queries this one. It is the spine.

ARCHITECTURE:
  Hybrid approach — one global manager + individual faction behavior classes.
  bw_SubFactionStateManager: the single queryable source of truth. Every other
    system asks this class what state a sub-faction is in. Never query
    individual faction classes directly from outside systems.
  bw_[FactionName]StateBehavior: each sub-faction has its own class that
    defines its transition logic. Clean separation. Black Keel's logic cannot
    accidentally affect Grovebound's logic.
  State enum: DORMANT / ACTIVE / ESCALATED / CRISIS
  Transition engine: CONDITIONS unlock escalation possibility.
    TIME drives it forward if unchecked.
  De-escalation: some sub-factions can be pushed back, others cannot.

PERMANENCE CATEGORIES:
  PERMANENT factions (cannot be eliminated through normal clan destruction):
    Black Keel — persistent pseudo-kingdom/custom-kingdom threat. Verify the
    correct local v1.4.5 clan/kingdom elimination hook before patching.
    Ironjarls — permanent Nord political dissident faction, not mercenary.
  SUPPRESSIBLE factions (can be pushed back, re-emerge if conditions return):
    All other sub-factions. Defeat sets them back. Conditions bring them back.

--- BLACK KEEL STATE TRANSITIONS ---
  DORMANT → ACTIVE: Pure fixed timer. Fires regardless of world conditions.
    Hrothgar and all captains hidden in fog of war throughout DORMANT.
    No parties. No presence. Player has no idea they exist.
    Looter-style small parties (15-40 troops) begin spawning on northern
    coasts using the bandit spawn system when ACTIVE fires.
    Lord parties do NOT appear yet during ACTIVE.
  ACTIVE → ESCALATED: Second fixed timer after going ACTIVE.
    Lord parties emerge. Named captains roam northern coast and sea lanes.
    Behave like minor faction lords. Grow in size over time.
    Hrothgar STILL hidden. World notification parchment card fires.
  ESCALATED → CRISIS: Third fixed timer.
    Hrothgar reveals himself. World event fires.
    Black Keel seizes HVALVIK specifically via a Bannerwake wrapper around
    ChangeOwnerOfSettlementAction.
    Garrison set to approximately 2,000 troops programmatically.
    AI patched to prevent casual NPC siege of Hvalvik.
    Hrothgar's party appears at Hvalvik.
    Settlement becomes Black Keel stronghold: their culture, their troops.
  RECONSTITUTION: If defeated at any state, lord parties go quiet for
    recovery period then re-emerge. Looter-style spawns NEVER fully stop.
    If Hvalvik retaken, resets to ESCALATED. Timer restarts. They return.
  SPAWN SYSTEM: Two parallel systems running simultaneously from ACTIVE:
    1. Looter-style spawns: 15-40 troops, bandit spawn system, always present
    2. Lord-style parties: named captain clans, minor faction lord behavior

--- LAKE RATS STATE TRANSITIONS ---
  DORMANT → ACTIVE: Early campaign timer. Small wrecker parties on Lake
    Laconis. Harbormaster not visible.
  ACTIVE → ESCALATED: Player-inactivity timer. If no action taken against
    Lake Rats in X days, formal toll operation fires. Any ship on Lake Laconis
    pays tribute or is attacked.
  CAP: Lake Rats cap at ESCALATED. No CRISIS state. Regional criminal
    operation, not a world threat.
  DE-ESCALATION: Player can destroy the beacon network through a specific
    quest. Pushes back to ACTIVE. They rebuild beacons over time. Returns to
    ESCALATED if ignored again.

--- JOMSVIKINGR STATE TRANSITIONS ---
  Always ACTIVE from campaign start. Mercenary brotherhood.
  ACTIVE → ESCALATED: Nordvyg loses significant territory (tracked by C#
    variable). Jomsvikingr declares independence, fortifies their hall as
    permanent independent entity.
  No CRISIS state. Political escalation only, not a threat arc.

--- GROVEBOUND STATE TRANSITIONS ---
  DORMANT → ACTIVE: Early-mid campaign. Groveseers begin appearing as
    traveling NPCs. Fighter parties appear in deep Battanian forest.
  ACTIVE → ESCALATED: Sacred site disturbed OR Battanian territory loss
    begins. Parties grow. Groveseers more visible.
  ESCALATED → CRISIS (THE GATHERING): Fires when Battania crosses a critical
    threshold — too many settlements lost OR a sacred site fully destroyed.
    Overwhelming temporary wave descends on responsible party/settlement.
    Returns to ESCALATED after the wave passes.
    THE GATHERING IS BATTANIA'S EXISTENTIAL DEFENSE MECHANISM.
    Fires multiple times per campaign — as many times as the threshold is
    crossed. Minimum cooldown between Gatherings prevents spam.
    The old gods don't let Battania die. This is mechanically enforced.
    Gathering Warriors do NOT appear in normal spawn pools. Event only.

--- KARAKHERGIT STATE TRANSITIONS ---
  DORMANT → ACTIVE: Fires when Khuzait begins losing territory (C# variable

SECTION 9a: MORTIA — VRAK THE FOUL, RAT-KING OF VLANDIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  NOT a campaign event. A full anti-Vlandian evil faction with settlements.
  Kingdom name: Mortia. Single-word, Vlandian-adjacent, wrong in the mouth,
    not a noble house name. Mortia is what Vrak calls the seized territory and
    what frightened merchants learn to write on ledgers when they cannot call
    it Vlandia anymore.
  Title: Rat-King Vrak the Foul
  Culture: Vlandian — the dark mirror of everything Vlandia pretends to be

  ORIGIN:
    Former high-ranking Vlandian military officer. Not a grunt — someone who
    understood logistics, supply chains, fortress vulnerabilities, troop
    movement. Corrupt from the inside. Got caught embezzling supply convoys.
    Sentenced to execution. Was being transported when a Woodsmen of Robynn
    ambush hit the convoy — chaos, burning wagons, guards routing. Vrak walked
    away. He owes his life to Robynn of the Wood without Robynn knowing or
    intending it. The Woodsmen of Robynn inadvertently created the Rat-King.
    Built his organization using military knowledge — the exact supply routes
    and garrison weaknesses he spent twenty years defending, now weaponized.

  FACTION IDENTITY:
    The dark mirror of Vlandia. Where Vlandia is gallant, radiant, knightly —
    Vrak is foul, calculating, and shameless. Same territory, same cultural
    roots, completely inverted values. His men don't follow him out of loyalty
    or admiration. They follow because he pays, because he's never lost yet,
    and because they're more afraid of what he'd do if they left than what his
    enemies would do if they stayed. He is a bastard who won. That's what
    makes him dangerous. Not a campaign event — he holds a small number of
    Vlandian border settlements from early campaign, expanding when Vlandia
    is distracted. Every castle he holds has a tunnel out.

  PERSONALITY — THE RAT-KING: STRONGHOLD + SKAVEN CALIBRATION:
    Primary references:
      Stronghold Crusader's Duc de Puce — The Rat. Hysterical, twitchy,
      arrogant that collapses when threatened, loud then suddenly quiet.
      Skaven from Total War: Warhammer — the underlying psychology without
      the fantasy vocabulary. Paranoid, self-aggrandizing, takes credit for
      everything, blames others for everything, convinced everyone is plotting
      against him (in Vrak's case this is probably accurate).
    Vrak is NOT honest. He is a Rat. He lies, deflects, takes credit for
    things he didn't do, blames others for things he did. What makes him
    different from Vlandian nobility is not honesty — it's that his
    dishonesty is naked and functional rather than dressed up as virtue.
    His men find this preferable to the alternative. Slightly.
    Hysterical, loud, ill-tempered underneath a calculating exterior.
    Arrogant about his intelligence, deeply insecure about everything else.
    Jittery, twitchy — the body betrays what the voice tries to conceal.
    Stutter appears specifically under pressure — forced to admit weakness,
    genuinely threatened, when the calculation slips and the anger shows.
    Finds things funny at wrong moments. Laughs when nothing is funny.
    Never raises his voice when genuinely threatening. Goes very quiet.
    That silence is the tell. When Vrak goes quiet, someone is about to die.
    Refers to Vlandian nobility as "those people" — contemptuous, personal.
    Never by title. Calls everyone "friend" when he means the opposite.
    Military vocabulary slips through occasionally. He catches it. That's
    more unsettling than if he didn't.
    He can't stop talking. Talking is how he controls a room. Silence makes
    him nervous. He is the most talkative named character in Bannerwake.
    Takes credit compulsively — if something succeeded, he planned it.
    Deflects blame compulsively — if something failed, someone else did it.
    Paranoid certainty that everyone is plotting against him at all times.
    His dialogue captures Skaven psychology in human speech:
      "I planned that. I planned— I planned all of that. The timing, the—
      the approach, the whole— yes. My plan. Hund suggested the flanking
      movement but that was— I had already thought of that. He just said it
      first. Because I told him to think about flanking. So. My plan."
      "You think— mmm. You think I don't know what you're doing? I know—
      I know what you're doing. I know what everyone is doing. I've always
      known. That's why I'm— [twitches] — that's why I'm still here and
      they're not."
    He is not admirable. He is effective. There is a difference. His men
    follow him because he keeps them alive, fed, and paid — not because
    they respect him. Sir Hund dey Brunval follows him not because Vrak
    is honest but because Vrak's dishonesty is at least legible. You can
    see Vrak's lies forming. That's better than noble lies you can't see.

  VISUAL IDENTITY — MILITARY THEATRICAL BANDIT:
    Helmet: Hound bascinet retextured — nose slightly rose-pink with engraved
      whiskers on the visor. Rat facial features worked into the metal.
      Not obvious at distance. Wrong up close. The player fights him and
      thinks about the helmet afterward. The player never sees his face.
    Armor: Old Vlandian officer armor, heraldry scratched off completely.
      Trophies added over years — pauldron from a lord he killed, cloak in
      no faction's color. Looks almost legitimate from distance. Up close
      every element has been modified. He erased himself from the record
      and built something new over the top of it.
    Banner: Brown and yellow. No noble device. Just color. The banner of
      men who wanted a banner.
    Silhouette: Military theatrical bandit. Officer's posture, officer's
      bearing, criminal's eyes. The contrast is deeply unsettling.

  ARMY COMPOSITION — HALF ATTEMPTING TO BE MILITARY:
    Outer layer — the mob: Standard looters and forest bandits. No loyalty
      beyond Vrak's presence. Cannon fodder who die cheap and replace cheap.
    Middle layer — criminal specialists: Mountain bandits, deserters, former
      soldiers who made worse choices than Vrak. Equipment, experience, hold
      a line when told. Not elite. Competent and committed to surviving.
    Inner core — personal guard: His officer knowledge applied to selection
      and training. Best looted equipment, fight like soldiers, personally
      loyal to Vrak specifically. When Vrak is defeated and driven out they
      scatter immediately — loyal to him, not the cause.
    The army looks organized at distance. Up close: chaos with structure
    underneath. When the veteran core breaks the whole thing dissolves.

SECTION 10: KARMA SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not a binary good/evil slider. A multi-axis behavioral reputation system that
tracks what kind of person the player is across all encounters and decisions.

FOUR AXES (each -100 to +100, tracked independently):
  Mercy / Cruelty: how the player treats the vulnerable, defeated, helpless
  Honor / Cunning: whether the player keeps their word, fights fair, or exploits
  Generosity / Greed: what the player does when they could take more than needed
  Faith / Pragmatism: respect for traditions, oaths, beliefs vs treating as tools

No axis is inherently good or bad:
  High Cruelty: certain factions fear the player productively
  High Cunning: Cult of Kosmos respects the player, opens dialogue options
  High Faith: the Grovebound tolerate player presence in Battanian territory,
    Groveseer may initiate contact
  High Honor: the Last Legion treats the player differently, Golden Boar
    offers better contract terms
  High Mercy: Woodsmen of Robynn approach the player, prisoner recruits more
    willing to submit to service

KARMA READS SUB-FACTION STATE:
  Sub-factions read player karma scores. A player with high Mercy and Generosity
  is approached differently by the Woodsmen of Robynn than a high Cruelty/Greed
  player. The Ironjarls respect demonstrated strength — a player who defeats Halthdar
  in battle earns their attention. The Dust Speakers read Pragmatism as
  disrespect for the sacred.

KARMA MODIFIES EVENTS:
  The same encounter fires differently based on karma axes. A wounded soldier
  reads the player differently if they're known for mercy vs. abandonment.
  Some event options only appear at certain karma thresholds.
  NPCs in settlements comment on reputation through overheard dialogue, a
  notable who looks differently, a merchant who locks his cart.
  No system message. No UI indicator. The world just knows.

IMPLEMENTATION:
  bw_KarmaSystem C# class. Four float variables stored per campaign save.
  Modified by encounter event outcomes, dialogue choices, combat behavior
  (attacking civilians, releasing vs. executing prisoners, etc.)
  Queried by: event system, sub-faction state system, dialogue trees,
  NPC relationship modifiers.

SECTION 11: EVENT SYSTEM — WORLD EVENTS AND ENCOUNTER EVENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is one of the most important systems in Bannerwake. The D&D feel of the
world, the CK3-style storytelling, the sense that the world is alive and
responding to the player — all of this lives here.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES:
  1. Two distinct event types working together — see below
  2. All events are pre-written authored prose. NO procedural generation.
     NO AI text generation. Every word is written by a human.
  3. CK3-quality writing standard: specific named perspectives, sensory
     detail, unanswered questions, faction-appropriate voice
  4. No game-mechanical language in event text. Never "Sub-faction X has
     entered ACTIVE state." Always pure in-world prose.
  5. Events must feel rarely repeatable across hundreds of hours of gameplay
  6. Events are interconnected with sub-faction dynamic state system —
     they read current world state and modify accordingly

--- EVENT TYPE 1: WORLD EVENTS ---
  The player hears about things through the world's communication channels.
  Framed as information reaching the player, not player witnessing directly.
  A merchant who passed through tells what he saw. A letter arrives. A notable
  in a tavern is talking loudly. The player is never magically present.
  The world has its own news network. The player taps into it.

  Triggers: OnSettlementEntered, daily campaign tick, NPC dialogue hooks
  Frequency: Every 3-5 days in settlements (minor), tied to conditions (major)
  Format: Parchment card with illustration and authored flavor text

  DISCOVERY EVENTS (every early-active sub-faction):
    Fires within first 30 days. Introduces the faction to the player.
    Framed as information the player encounters naturally — never as a
    system notification. See example discovery texts below.

--- EVENT TYPE 2: ENCOUNTER EVENTS (D&D STYLE) ---
  The player is on the road, at sea, or in a settlement and something happens
  to them specifically. Direct interactions with genuine choices.
  Every encounter has:
    - Specific situation in authored prose
    - 3-4 choices with genuinely different outcomes
    - No correct answer — the world is morally complex
    - Consequences that ripple: sometimes immediately, sometimes days later
    - Connected to larger world — seemingly random events tied to sub-factions

  Triggers: local confirmed hooks plus Bannerwake polling/wrappers:
    OnSettlementEntered / BeforeSettlementEntered / AfterSettlementEntered,
    DailyTick, HourlyTick, DailyTickParty, DailyTickSettlement,
    OnPlayerBattleEnd, OnSettlementOwnerChanged, naval blockade/ship events,
    and custom travel-condition polling while the main party moves.
    Do not use unconfirmed hook names such as OnPlayerTravel or
    OnPlayerEncounterEvent as direct API calls unless source audit later proves
    they exist in this install.
  Frequency: Minor encounters every 5-10 days, major every 15-20 days
  These frequencies are calibrated to feel special, not routine.

--- EVENT MODIFIER LAYERS ---
  Every base event is modified by multiple layers simultaneously:
  State modifier: which sub-factions are in which states changes event meaning
  Karma modifier: player's axes shift options available and NPC behavior
  World state modifier: current wars, prosperity levels, faction strength
  Seasonal modifier: winter events feel different from summer events
  Location modifier: same base event fires differently in different regions

  A "merchant in trouble" event has potentially dozens of variations when
  all modifiers stack. The player may encounter the same base situation twice
  in 200 hours and have a completely different experience both times.

--- EXAMPLE DISCOVERY EVENT TEXTS (CK3 STYLE) ---

  Lake Rats:

  Lake Laconis, three days past its scheduled arrival at Varcheg. The crew
  was gone. The cargo was gone. The lantern on the prow had been moved —
  someone had hung it facing the wrong direction. The harbor master in Omor
  says the lake has always had its dangers. He says this quickly and doesn't
  meet your eyes."

  Woodsmen of Robynn:

  The Renegati:

--- TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION ---
  Campaign behavior hooked to confirmed CampaignEvents and Bannerwake wrapper
  events: settlement entry, daily/hourly ticks, party/settlement ticks,
  player battle end, settlement owner changes, war/peace state changes,
  blockade/ship ownership/ship destruction events, and custom travel polling.
  Each event is a pre-written XML event with authored text, branching
  outcomes, and consequence flags stored as campaign variables.
  Consequence flags can affect the world days or weeks later.
  Event system reads from bw_SubFactionStateManager for state modifiers.
  Event system reads from bw_KarmaSystem for karma modifiers.
  Event pool is large enough that repetition is rare across 200+ hours.
  Events are weighted by region, season, world state, and faction activity.

SECTION 12b: NORDS — THE KINGDOM OF NORDVYG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nordvyg is twenty years old. This is the most important thing about it. Twenty
years ago there were twenty petty kings. Volbjorn the Hungry spent a decade in
the Empire as a Vaegar Guard — accumulating wealth, studying how civilizations
are built, understanding what Nordvyg could be. He returned and built it. Some
petty kings were bought. Some were outmaneuvered. Some were defeated in battle.
Some did the calculation themselves and bent the knee before the reckoning came.

Halthdar inherited a kingdom that works. His problem is that it works because
his father made it work, and Volbjorn's tools — personal terror, overwhelming
pressure, the implicit threat of what he was willing to do — are not Halthdar's
tools. Halthdar is better at governance than his father. He is better at
diplomacy. He is not frightening in the same way, and some people who bent the
knee because of that specific quality are noticing the difference.

--- HALTHDAR DEY THROSNIRING — THE GOLDEN KING ---

  XML traits: Generous, Daring, Impulsive (Calculating -1).
  The Golden is his hair and his father's name for him. He took the name and
  made it mean something different — not wealth but aspiration.

  What he does well: Governance. Halthdar is genuinely good at administration,
    at finding the settlement that every clan chief can accept without anyone
    being fully satisfied, at making the unified kingdom feel like a continuous
    choice rather than a historical fact imposed by force.

  What he does badly: His father's enemies did not fear Halthdar the way they
    feared Volbjorn. The Ironjarls know this. They are not afraid of Halthdar.
    Grimulf attends his feasts, sits in the correct place, says the correct
    things. He has never technically broken his oath. Neither has any Ironjarl.
    They are counting every time Halthdar is not his father.

  His impulsiveness (Calculating -1) is the one way he resembles Volbjorn —
    he makes decisions quickly, commits before the political calculation is
    complete. Sometimes this is the right call. Sometimes it gives the Ironjarls
    something new to count.

  The Jomsvikingr relationship: Halthdar respects Floki because Floki is the
    kind of man Volbjorn would have respected, which is its own kind of
    complication. The Jomsvikingr's hall predates Nordvyg by generations.
    Halthdar's kingdom was built over something that was already there. He
    acknowledges this by leaving them alone. They acknowledge it by not causing
    problems. This arrangement is stable until Nordvyg loses enough territory
    that the Jomsvikingr calculate independence is worth more than neutrality.

  The Black Keel question: Halthdar is aware something is wrong with Hrothgar's
    crew. He has been aware since the first reports came back from fishing boats.
    He does not know what it is. Nobody does. He has considered that the
    Jomsvikingr might know — Floki's intelligence network covers the northern
    coast extensively. He has not asked Floki directly. He is not certain he
    wants the answer Floki would give.

  Dialogue voice: Daring and direct. He does not perform the elaborate Vlandian-
    style political positioning. He says what he means, asks what he needs, and
    makes decisions in front of you. High karma players (Honor, Valor) earn his
    genuine respect. Players who maneuver politically around him earn his
    wariness. He notices the difference.

--- THE NINE CLANS OF NORDVYG ---

  THROSNIRING — The Royal Clan
    Leader: Halthdar
    Members: Ilrika, Halthdar, Jarminja, Mjalrik, Sidunric, Thyrsif, Orvi,

    Holdings: Thronderlag (capital), Skarthness Castle
    Character: The ruling clan contains two capable and ambitious members in
      Mjalrik and Sidunric — both understand what the kingdom is worth and both
      have opinions about how to govern it. Neither has challenged Halthdar
      openly. Both are watching the Ironjarls the same way the Ironjarls are
      watching Halthdar — counting failures, remaining technically loyal, waiting
      for the calculus to shift.

  KJOLDING — The Sea Clan
    Leader: Grykka
    Members: Grykka, Gautgar, Iridrun, Heimkir, Agrynja, Dyfa
    Holdings: Gretysfjord, Tharklif Castle
    Character: Grykka earned her position through twenty years of sea-raiding.
      She is one of the hardest-bitten clan leaders in Nordvyg — not cruel, but
      impatient with weakness and deeply unimpressed by those who haven't paid
      the same price. Currently loyal to Halthdar because his governance is
      competent and his protection of sea lanes benefits her clan directly.
      If Halthdar's competence becomes less obvious, Grykka reassesses. She has
      made pragmatic calculations her entire career. This is another one.
      Black Keel angle: Grykka has more sea-going intelligence than any other
      clan leader. She has heard things from her fishing fleet about Black Keel

    Leader: Asgotha
    Members: Asgotha, Vulthir, Kjarvon, Aelfeyja, Njasnir

    Character: Asgotha is the single most plot-relevant clan leader in Nordvyg.

    Members: Gafnir, Vitharsura, Karlek, Ulvirinja, Olvira
    Holdings: Ykerslund Castle
    Character: Gafnir got his position through Halthdar's direct appointment —

      in a position that should have been determined by traditional succession.
      The resentment is quiet. It comes out in small ways at The Thing.
      If Halthdar is politically weakened: Gafnir's position becomes vulnerable.

    Leader: Murin
    Members: Unjort, Tovsja, Gudrinja, Svalsa, Orthogar, Liljan, Murin
    Holdings: Ulikshorn Castle
    Character: Murin is old enough to remember Volbjorn's wars as a young man.

    Leader: Toverik
    Members: Toverik, Hvana
    Holdings: Brunmark Castle
    Character: The smallest clan in Nordvyg with only two confirmed members.

  VISDURING — The Isolated Clan
    Leader: Dagvi
    Members: Dagvi, Drivana, Otfar, Anle, Triven
    Holdings: Fimbulgard Castle (most isolated castle in Nordvyg)
    Character: Dagvi's clan has held Fimbulgard's geographic position since before
      Volbjorn. They survive through geography and the strategy of not making
      enemies. Three political regimes have washed over Nordvyg. Visduring is
      still there afterward, correctly aligned, having offended no one who ended
      up winning. Dagvi is not passive. He is strategically invisible.
      If a player needs something from Nordvyg's northern inland region and wants
      a reliable point of contact outside the Ironjarl/Halthdar political drama,
      Dagvi is the correct choice.

--- NORDVYG POLITICAL WEB ---

  Three distinct Nord power centers, three distinct relationships to the kingdom:

  HALTHDAR'S NORDVYG: The unified kingdom trying to become more than its origin.
    Dependent on Halthdar's personal authority and his ability to govern well
    enough that the costs of unification feel worth it. Strong when he is strong.
    Fragile when he shows sustained weakness.

  THE IRONJARLS: The permanent fracture line. Grimulf is not waiting for a
    specific event — he is waiting for a sufficient accumulation of failures.
    There is no single battle Halthdar can win that closes this threat. There is
    no specific grievance he can address that resolves it. The Ironjarls exist
    because some people's fathers were kings and those people have not forgotten
    it. That condition only ends when they run out of patience or alternatives.

  THE JOMSVIKINGR: Independent of both. Floki's hall predates Nordvyg. The
    Jomsvikingr's independence is not political — it is older than the politics.
    They stay out of Ironjarl/Halthdar dynamics deliberately. The code prohibits
    taking positions in internal political conflicts; mercenary neutrality is both
    rule and doctrine. They can be hired by either side. This has happened. It
    will happen again.

  HOW THEY INTERACT:
    Ironjarls vs. Halthdar: A cold war with formal compliance. Every Ironjarl
      attends Halthdar's events. None have technically violated their oaths.
      The pressure is real. The conflict is latent. It erupts when Nordvyg
      loses enough.
    Jomsvikingr neutrality: Floki has been offered a position in Nordvyg's
      formal military structure. He declined. In language that was technically
      respectful. This is the most pointed possible rejection and everyone
      present understood it.
    Black Keel — the third variable: Black Keel is not part of Nordvyg. They
      are not Ironjarls. They predate even the Jomsvikingr's current arrangement.
      Whatever Hrothgar is now, he was once something else. Who he was before —
      his relationship to the north, to specific clan leaders, to the waters he
      sailed — is a question the player can investigate but never fully answer.

--- BLACK KEEL AND THE NORD CLANS ---

  The seizure of Hvalvik is the most specific political wound in Nordvyg.
  Asgotha holds that town. The political consequences cascade:

  Halthdar: Cannot let this stand. Cannot immediately address it. The garrison
    Black Keel installs at Hvalvik is enormous. Any campaign against it requires
    Nord clans to commit together in a way that the Ironjarl dynamic makes
    difficult. He is trapped between the need to respond and the political cost
    of the coalition required.

  Grimulf: Notes that Halthdar cannot protect a coastal town from pirates. Does
    not say this publicly. Says it at small dinners with people already thinking
    it.

  Grykka: Has known something was wrong with Black Keel crews for months. When
    Hvalvik falls she becomes the most important voice in Nord war councils.
    Her intelligence about northern sea conditions is unmatched. She has
    conditions for contributing her fleet. The conditions are specific. They
    involve her clan's political status. She is not wrong to attach them.

  Asgotha: The one who lost it. His subsequent state — whether he is broken by
    it, sharpened by it, or transformed by what he's seen — is the most
    important individual character arc in Nordvyg. The player's relationship
    with Asgotha determines how this plays out. A player who listened before
    Hvalvik fell is already in a different relationship than one who shows up
    after the crisis to help recapture the town. Asgotha notices the difference.

SECTION 12c: BATTANIA — THE OLD KINGDOMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Battania is not one thing. It is eight clans that have been agreeing on certain
things since before anyone can remember, disagreeing on everything else, and
calling the combination a kingdom when outsiders need a word for it. The Ca fál
is the architecture — the religious-legal framework that makes Battanian political
cooperation possible without requiring a strong central government. Caladog is
the king. He governs by the Ca fál's rules more than by his own authority. This
is both the source of his legitimacy and the permanent limit of his power.

--- CALADOG FEN MORRIGAN — KING OF BATTANIA ---

  He was chosen by the Grove Council, not born to the position. This is the
  most important fact about him. He was selected because the eight clan chiefs
  agreed he was the best available option for keeping the Battanian clans from
  tearing each other apart long enough to present a unified face to three
  separate Empire factions, each of which wants different pieces of Battanian
  territory for different reasons.

  Caladog knows exactly why he was chosen. He has been king for eleven years
  by making himself indispensable — not through military dominance, not through
  political maneuvering, but through being the one person no other clan chief
  can argue is unnecessary. That is a fragile kind of authority. It requires
  continuous maintenance.

  What he actually believes: The Ca fál is real. Not performatively — actually
    real. He was a Gwylwatcher before he was a political figure; he spent years
    in deep forest training, learning the tradition from inside it. He understands
    Ca fál theology and law from genuine practice, not political convenience. This
    is his greatest political asset (the Grovebound respect him as someone who
    has done the work) and his greatest personal constraint (he cannot violate
    Ca fál principles without losing the legitimacy that comes from that respect,
    and he genuinely does not want to).

  His relationship with Brenach fen Ashyr: Mutual respect, genuine tension.
    Brenach believes Caladog has made too many pragmatic compromises. Caladog
    believes Brenach has the luxury of purity because he doesn't have to govern
    eight clans simultaneously. Both are correct. This is never said directly.
    It doesn't need to be.

  His relationship with the Empire: Caladog is not afraid of the Empire. He
    is exhausted by it. Three factions, each making territorial demands, each
    offering different and contradictory arrangements, each trying to use
    Battanian territory as buffer or prize in their internal conflict. He has
    spent eleven years being courted by all three while giving none of them what
    they want. The Grove Council supports this approach. It is the correct
    approach. It is also interminable.

  Dialogue: Caladog speaks carefully and at length. He is the only major king
    who quotes Ca fál precedent in political conversations — not to impress, but
    because he thinks in those terms. High Faith karma players can follow these
    references. He asks hard questions and waits for real answers. A player who
    gives him a real answer, even an unwelcome one, earns more from him than
    one who performs the expected deference.

--- CA FÁL AS POLITICAL STRUCTURE ---

  The Ca fál is Battania's constitution. It predates every current clan chief,
  every current king, every current political arrangement. It does not change
  quickly. It does not change because one person argues for the change.

  The Grove Council: The Ca fál's primary political institution. All eight clan
    chiefs hold seats. Groveseers attend as observers with speaking rights but
    not votes. Major decisions require supermajority — six of eight clans must
    agree for declaring war, enacting formal treaties, changing sacred-site
    status. Four clans can veto any major action.

  Sacred sites: Specific locations throughout Battanian territory hold Ca fál
    designation. The grove outside Dunglanys is the most significant — it holds
    the year's most important rite. Attacking a sacred site is not a political
    act in Battanian understanding; it is a crime against the gods themselves.
    The Gathering is the Ca fál's enforcement mechanism.

  Groveseers' travel rights: Any Groveseer may travel through any territory
    in Calradia without hindrance, by ancient law that no one officially
    recognizes but most find too costly to violate. A Groveseer harmed in an
    enemy city is a political crisis for that city, not for Battania. This has
    happened twice in living memory. Both times it ended badly for the city.

  Ca fál law and mercy: The Ca fál has specific provisions about mercy in battle,
    treatment of prisoners, and what constitutes legitimate war. These are not
    suggestions. Battanian lords who violate them face Grove Council review.
    Caladog has had to enforce this twice, against clan chiefs whose military
    victories he needed. He enforced it anyway. This is why the Grovebound
    respect him.

--- THE BATTANIAN CLANS ---

  Eight clans hold Battanian territory. Their personalities shape Caladog's
  governance as much as any decision he makes. Full clan rosters and individual
  character development to be authored as each clan receives full treatment.
  Priority order for authoring: fen Morrigan, fen Uvain, fen Eingal, fen Morcar,
  then remaining four clans in order of NPC reference confirmation.

  FEN MORRIGAN — The Royal Clan
    Leader: Caladog
    Holds: Dunglanys (primary sacred site). The most politically significant
      Battanian settlement. Whoever holds it holds the most important ritual
      location in Battania. Caladog has never permitted a non-Battanian to
      attend the Dunglanys rites. He has been asked twice. His refusal both
      times was not hostile. It was complete.

  FEN UVAIN — The Forest Clan
    Confirmed members: Culharn [lord_5_9] (Honest, Impulsive), Melidir
      [lord_5_5] (Generous, Devious, Cautious), Alcaea [lord_5_6] (Cautious,
      Empire culture note — pending authoring)
    Melidir's trait combination (Generous + Devious + Cautious) suggests someone
      who wants the best for his clan and has made compromises to achieve it.
      The combination of generosity and deviousness is not contradiction — it is
      a specific political type: someone who genuinely wants good outcomes and
      has decided that means doing things that look bad. Whether he is right
      is a question the player can explore through direct interaction.
    Culharn remains active fen Uvain unless a specific authored event changes
      him. Do NOT write him as Grovebound without an explicit event.

  FEN EINGAL — The Northern Clan
    Confirmed members: Taorse [lord_5_21_2] (age 18, Honest), Wythuin (Tier-1
      priority character), Brighan [lord_5_17_1] (Calculating)
    Taorse is young, softspoken, honorable — three traits that in Battanian
      politics represent genuine idealism that has not yet been tested by
      governance. She is Carfyd and Beathag's daughter. Her trajectory will
      be shaped by the events she witnesses in the coming decade. She is among
      the most interesting Battanian characters to author precisely because she
      has not yet become whatever she will become.
    CORRECTION LOCK: Taorse is NOT the Grovebound elder. She is an active young
      noble. Grovebound elder content uses Bannerwake-invented names only.

  FEN MORCAR — The Eastern Clan
    Confirmed member: Branoc [lord_5_18] (spouse Seonag, son of Pryndor)
    CORRECTION LOCK: Branoc is NOT a Grovebound leader. Active fen Morcar noble.
    Full clan roster pending NPC reference confirmation.

  Remaining four clans: Pending full roster confirmation from NPC reference.
    Do not invent clan names; confirm from the authoritative NPC reference file
    before authoring any character.

--- THE GROVEBOUND AND REGULAR BATTANIA ---

  The Grovebound are not separate from Battanian society. They are its deepest
  expression — the people who took the Ca fál seriously enough to observe it
  completely. Every Battanian knows what the Grovebound are. Most Battanians
  have a complex relationship with them: respect for the commitment, discomfort
  with the extremity, gratitude that someone is doing it rather than everyone
  having to. The Grovebound don't find this gratitude satisfying. They don't
  do it to be thanked.

  Brenach fen Ashyr has not left the sacred grove in forty years. He has more
  political influence than any clan chief who hasn't visited Caladog in a year.
  The grove is his parliament. Clan chiefs come to him.

  Caladog's relationship with Brenach is Battania's defining political dynamic
  — the tension between governing pragmatically and maintaining the Ca fál's
  integrity. Caladog manages this tension. He does not resolve it. It cannot be
  resolved. It is the condition of governing Battania. Any writer authoring
  Battanian content should understand this tension and write from inside it.

SECTION 12a: VLANDIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Vlandia is the most politically complex faction in Bannerwake. Internal dissent
is canonical, Derthert is explicitly weak, the barons are explicitly fractious.
The goal is to deepen every named character into a full person with specific
motivations, private behavior, and a defined relationship to the world around
them. Every Vlandian lord should feel like they have been living their life
before the player arrived.

--- DERTHERT DEY MEROC — KING OF VLANDIA ---
  Personality: Genuinely good but politically outmaneuvered. A decent man in
    an impossible position. Not weak through complacency or ignorance — he
    sees exactly what is happening and has run out of ways to stop it without
    becoming something he doesn't want to be. His world-weariness is not
    resignation. It is the specific exhaustion of a man who has been right
    about everything for twenty years and watched it make no difference.
  What he knows: More than anyone gives him credit for. His read of political
    situations is accurate. His instincts are correct. None of this matters
    because Vlandian kingship requires being feared, and Derthert is not
    frightening to anyone.
  The Ingalther-Aldric dynamic: He has watched these two barons pull in
    opposite directions for fifteen years. He uses it occasionally — lets one
    press too far, watches the other push back, steps in as arbiter and gains
    temporary authority. It works. It is also slowly destroying Vlandia because
    the kingdom is permanently in managed internal tension rather than building
    toward anything.
  The Vrak humiliation: Losing Galend — his own clan's town — to a criminal
    king is the most humiliating thing that has happened to his reign. He sent
    two punitive expeditions. Both failed because Aldric and Ingalther refused
    to contribute troops to the same campaign simultaneously.
  Private behavior — FERRAND:
    Derthert has one private advisor nobody knows about. A traveling cloth
    merchant named Ferrand, fifty-three years old, who has been moving goods
    between Vlandian settlements for thirty years. Ferrand reports to Derthert
    in exchange for quiet protection of his trade routes. He hears barons
    speaking candidly, garrison commanders complaining, village headmen
    describing conditions. He reports without filter or agenda.
    Ferrand knew about Vrak's criminal enterprise three months before Galend
    fell. He told Derthert. Derthert did not act in time because he didn't
    know who to trust with the information. By the time he decided, Galend
    was gone. Ferrand has not mentioned this since. He continues reporting.
    He considers it his job, not his problem.
    Ferrand's wife lives in Rovalt. She knows about the arrangement. She has
    never told anyone. She considers it reasonable.
    CULT OF KOSMOS CONNECTION: Ferrand recently reported a specific merchant in
    three different Vlandian cities asking odd questions about shipping routes
    and coastal access. He told Derthert six weeks ago. Derthert doesn't know
    what to make of it. A player who has assembled enough Kosmos fragments
    will understand immediately what Ferrand saw.
  Dialogue: Derthert speaks carefully. He says less than he knows. He asks
    questions more often than he makes statements. He is trying to understand
    who the player is before deciding what to tell them. If the player has
    high Honor, Derthert opens more. If the player serves Ingalther or Aldric,
    Derthert is polite and reveals nothing.

--- INGALTHER DEY CORTAIN — THE MOST DANGEROUS BARON ---
  Personality: Ambitious beyond reason. Ruthless and calculating. Vanilla
    confirms he seized his estate from his own half-brother — the first
    thing he ever wanted, he took. Every acquisition since has followed
    the same logic.
  What he actually wants: Not the crown. The power behind the crown. A king
    has to perform, to be visible, to be accountable. The man behind the king
    has none of those constraints and all of the power. He has been building
    a network of obligation for years — every favor to a smaller baron creates
    a debt, every public support of Derthert spends credit privately.
  The Calatild obsession: Has been pressing Derthert to disinherit Calatild
    for years. Has a dynastic claim on Arromanc lands. Now Vrak holds Jaculan
    and Calatild is landless. This gives Ingalther three options:
      1. Ally with Vrak to legitimize his claim on Arromanc lands
      2. Use Vrak's seizure as justification to "reclaim" Jaculan himself
      3. Wait while Vrak weakens Derthert, Aldric, and Calatild simultaneously
    He is considering all three. He is in no hurry.
  The Hidden Hand connection: One of Ingalther's closer advisors carries a
    silver sigil under his collar. Whether Ingalther knows and finds it useful,
    or is genuinely unaware, the player can never be certain. Cult of Kosmos has
    been watching Vlandia's political destabilization with specific interest.
    A fractured Vlandia serves the arrangement.
  Dialogue: Smooth, formal, always positioned. He asks after the player's
    interests before offering his own. He remembers everything said to him
    in previous conversations. High Cunning players get a different Ingalther
    — he respects the architecture of other people's ambition.

--- ALDRIC DEY TIHR — THE SIMPLEST BARON ---
  Personality: Simply greedy. No ideology, no vision, no self-justification.
    He takes because taking is available. His subjects suffer because their
    suffering is the cost of his comfort. He would be genuinely puzzled by
    anyone who found this remarkable. This makes him oddly honest compared
    to Ingalther's elaborate positioning — there is no gap between Aldric's
    public face and his private one.
  The Vrak grievance: Lost Drapand Castle to Vrak's criminal enterprise.
    Wants Vrak driven out for property reasons, not principle. Will not cooperate
    with Ingalther to achieve this — would rather lose more territory than
    owe Ingalther a favor.
  His daughter Liena: The only dey Tihr member without negative traits.
    Vanilla confirms this. In Bannerwake this tension is explicit — she knows
    what her father is, stays because leaving would abandon Pravend's subjects
    to someone worse, has been quietly protecting individuals from his worst
    decisions for years without his knowledge. Aldric is vaguely aware she
    does things he doesn't approve of. He hasn't stopped her because she's
    useful and she's his.
  Dialogue: Direct and transactional. Asks what the player wants and what
    they're offering. Doesn't pretend to be something he isn't. Finds
    elaborate political positioning tedious. If the player is direct with
    him, he responds in kind. Rare among Vlandian lords.

--- CALATILD DEY ARROMANC — DISPLACED BARONESS ---
  Personality: Genuinely excellent at governance. Jaculan was prosperous
    under her precisely because she ran it. Acutely aware that excellence
    was the only thing protecting her from Ingalther's dynastic claim.
  Current situation: Landless. Attending every council session in Pravend,
    petitioning Derthert, maintaining court position despite having no fief
    to back it up. Running out of patience while performing patience perfectly.
    Derthert wants to help her. Cannot without a political fight he can't
    afford. Has been promising action for six months.
  The specific cruelty: Ingalther's dynastic claim strengthens the longer
    she is landless. A baroness with no fief is easier to disinherit than
    one with a functioning domain. Every day she waits is a day Ingalther's
    lawyers are working.
  What she believes: That Ingalther arranged the council session that kept
    her away from Jaculan when it fell — this is wrong. That Derthert was
    complicit in the timing — also wrong. The true answer (Gurn simply waited
    for any convenient opportunity and the council session was coincidentally
    convenient) is the most infuriating possible explanation and the true one.
    She has built an entire political framework on a wrong assumption.
  Her husband Unthery: Furious in a way she has asked him not to express
    publicly. He is not good at this. She manages him the way she managed
    Jaculan — with patience and specific instructions and the knowledge that
    he means well and would make everything worse if left to his own judgment.
  Dialogue: Precise and controlled. Says exactly what she means and no more.
    Asks the player specific questions about their faction allegiances before
    saying anything substantive. If the player is not allied with Ingalther,
    she opens significantly. She needs allies who don't want what Ingalther
    wants. She doesn't ask this directly. She creates situations where the
    player can demonstrate it.

--- FERRAND — DERTHERT'S EYES ---
  BANNERWAKE-INVENTED. Not a lord. Not a notable. A traveling cloth merchant.
  Physical: Fifty-three years old. Has the weathered look of a man who spends
    most of his life outdoors. Forgettable in the way people who are always
    around become forgettable — guards don't watch him, lords don't lower their
    voices, merchants assume he's a competitor and nothing more.
  Background: Has been on Vlandian roads since nineteen with his father's cart.
    Took over when his father died. Wife in Rovalt named Aldine — sees her four
    months a year. She knows about the arrangement. Finds it reasonable.
  Role: The only honest intelligence Derthert receives. Reports without filter
    or agenda. His interest is the arrangement — Derthert's quiet protection of
    his trade routes in exchange for what he sees and hears.
  Player encounter: The player can encounter Ferrand in any Vlandian settlement.
    He appears as a generic traveling merchant. He is not flagged as important.
    Only through specific dialogue choices — asking about road conditions,
    listening to what he says about specific settlements — does his unusual
    quality of information become apparent. He never reveals his arrangement
    with Derthert. If pressed, he talks about cloth prices.
  Cult of Kosmos observation: Ferrand has seen a specific merchant in three
    different Vlandian cities asking questions about shipping routes and coastal
    access that no legitimate merchant would need to ask. He told Derthert.
    If the player encounters Ferrand and asks the right questions, they can
    learn about this merchant — a Kosmos fragment acquired through a merchant
    conversation about cloth prices, which is exactly the kind of discovery
    the Kosmos fragment system should produce.

DYNAMIC CHARACTER LAYERS — VLANDIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CRITICAL MECHANICS NOTE FOR CLAUDE CODE:
Settlement capture ≠ kingdom collapse. Vrak's kingdom persists as long as
any settlement is held and any clan is sworn to it. Individual clan members
do not automatically defect when their king loses battles. Character behavior
changes are triggered by SPECIFIC conditions, not general weakness.

Correct triggers for behavior changes:
- Specific settlement captured (not all settlements)
- Specific character personally defeated in battle
- Kingdom loses ALL settlements → clans go independent
- Individual clan defection (requires relationship, leverage, bribing)
- Player power threshold crossed
- Specific world events (Cult of Kosmos revealed, Gathering fires, etc.)
- Specific quest completions

--- CALATILD DEY ARROMANC — DYNAMIC LAYERS ---

IF Galend is sieged by anyone:
  Calatild watches closely. Galend was Vrak's first seizure. If it falls
  to Vlandia she immediately petitions for consideration in any territorial
  redistribution — not for Galend, for whatever leverage she can extract
  from the moment. She has been waiting for any weakness in Vrak's position.

IF Vrak personally loses a major battle (not his kingdom, just a battle):
  She petitions Derthert for a campaign against Jaculan specifically while
  Vrak recovers. Offers her household cavalry. Has been waiting for exactly
  this vulnerability window. The petition is prepared — she wrote it months
  ago and has been waiting for the right moment to deliver it.

IF Vrak's kingdom collapses entirely and Jaculan becomes claimable:
  Calatild's petition moves from background noise to urgent foreground. She
  is physically present at Pravend every day. Legal documentation of her
  prior governance, prosperity records, testimony from village headmen.
  She argues her own case better than any lawyer because she lived it.
  If Ingalther claims Jaculan before her — she becomes a different kind of
  threat to Vlandian stability than Vrak ever was.

IF player takes Jaculan and grants it to Calatild:
  Formal acceptance. Gratitude real but controlled — she doesn't perform it.
  Six weeks later the player receives specific enemy intelligence they needed.
  It came through channels Calatild managed. She never mentions it. The
  player understands what happened.

IF Ingalther moves against her legally:
  Cumbert's eighteenth objection fails. Council called. Calatild drops the
  patient performance entirely — states in front of every Vlandian baron
  exactly what Ingalther is doing, why it serves only him, and what it will
  cost everyone when he has no more rivals to absorb. Parchment event card.
  Player can attend. Player presence and response shapes the outcome.

IF Derthert is replaced by any political succession path:
  All existing relationships reset. Calatild pivots immediately to
  demonstrating administrative value to whoever comes next — offers to govern
  contested territories, presents detailed plans for stabilizing Vrak-affected
  settlements. She is selling competence to whoever is buying.

--- VARTIN DEY JELIND — DYNAMIC LAYERS ---

IF Ingalther accepts Vartin's intelligence offer:
  Vartin gets subtle political backing. Ingalther mentions his restoration at
  council once, obliquely. Vartin's behavior shifts — more confident, more
  dangerous. He immediately uses this to undermine Aldmar dey Vornac's
  competing restoration petition. Two displaced barons, one restoration
  available. Vartin makes the comparison favor him. All dishonorably. None
  illegally.

IF Hongard Castle is taken from Vrak by anyone:
  Vartin is in Pravend within two days. If player took Hongard — Vartin
  approaches directly. Transactional pitch: support his restoration claim
  and he provides specific intelligence about which Vlandian lords have
  debts, secrets, vulnerabilities. He is offering to be useful in the way
  dishonorable men are useful.

IF nobody advocates for Vartin and months pass:
  He disappears from Pravend without announcement. World event notification:
  Vartin has been seen near Battanian border settlements. What he's doing
  there is unclear. The Grovebound have no use for dishonorable Vlandian
  barons. Someone else might.

IF player investigates his disappearance:
  Vartin has made contact with a specific traveling merchant. The player who
  has been tracking Kosmos fragments recognizes the description. Vartin dey
  Jelind has been approached by a Kosmos Face. What was offered is unknown.
  What Vartin agreed to is unknown. He has not returned to Pravend.

--- ALDMAR DEY VORNAC — DYNAMIC LAYERS ---

IF Talivel Castle is sieged by anyone including the player:
  Aldmar defends it. For himself, not for Vrak. Feudal self-interest —
  the castle is his fief, his income, his position. He fights any army that
  approaches regardless of his feelings about his king. There is no
  contradiction with his contempt for Vrak.

IF player builds high relationship with Aldmar through repeated encounters:
  He begins passing small pieces of information. Garrison schedules at Vrak's
  settlements. Supply route timing. Gurn's network contact points he has
  observed. Never overtly. Always embedded in other conversation. He is not
  defecting. He is leaving a door open.

IF Vrak's kingdom loses enough settlements to become critically weak:
  Aldmar approaches the player independently — not Derthert, the player.
  He has assessed who is capable of finishing what has started. He offers
  defection in exchange for specific terms: advocacy for restoration of his
  ORIGINAL Vlandian lands (not Talivel), and formal recognition that he
  served under duress. These terms are not significantly negotiable. He
  will be precise about them and mean them.

IF Aldmar defects and returns to Vlandia:
  His information about Vrak's operation is extensive and accurate — supply
  routes, garrison weaknesses, Gurn's Greypath entry points, Hund's
  defensive patterns, Vrak's behavior under pressure. Delivered to Derthert
  in formal audience. Campaign against remaining Vrak holdings accelerates.
  Aldmar receives credit he doesn't ask for and doesn't acknowledge.

IF Aldmar's original Vlandian rival still holds his original lands:
  Even after Vrak's collapse, his core grievance is unresolved. He circles
  that specific lord in Vlandian court politics. Player can assist, oppose,
  or stay neutral. Each choice has specific consequences for Vlandian
  political stability that affect other characters' situations.

--- ERDURAND DEY MEROC — DYNAMIC LAYERS ---

IF Derthert loses two or more settlements to any enemy:
  Murmuring barons become vocal. Ingalther approaches Erdurand through
  intermediaries — never directly at first. A dinner arranged through an
  aligned baron. Entirely social, completely political. Erdurand attends.
  He is gathering information about what's being offered before deciding
  what to do with it. World event parchment card: "The king's heir has
  been seen in private audience with associates of Baron Ingalther
  dey Cortain. The meeting was not announced."

IF player has high relationship with Derthert and is present in Pravend:
  Derthert mentions Erdurand carefully — not a complaint, not a request.
  "My son is being courted by men who want a different kind of king. He
  knows this. I believe he is using it rather than being used by it. I
  believe this because I need to believe it." Player can respond or stay
  silent. Derthert doesn't need an answer. He needed to say it.

IF player builds direct relationship with Erdurand:
  He is direct. Asks what the player wants from Vlandia before offering
  anything himself. Respects directness because he uses it. If player is
  honest about goals, he is honest about whether those goals align with his.
  He doesn't promise what he can't deliver. He doesn't threaten what he
  won't execute.

IF Erdurand becomes king through any political succession path:
  First decision: decisive, satisfies the barons.
  Second decision: decisive, satisfies nobody.
  Third decision: reveals he has been thinking about Vlandia's structural
    problems in ways nobody anticipated.
  Ingalther's patronage evaporates when Erdurand reveals he has no interest
  in being managed. Ingalther pivots immediately to containing the damage.
  Erdurand has been expecting this pivot. He prepared for it.
  It is still harder than he anticipated.

--- INGALTHER DEY CORTAIN — DYNAMIC LAYERS ---

IF Vrak is weakened significantly (not collapsed, just weakened):
  Ingalther immediately moves on Arromanc claim. Calatild is landless.
  Jaculan is contested. The moment is right. Formal legal documentation
  filed with the crown. Cumbert's procedural objections begin failing.
  World event fires. Player can intervene or observe. Each choice shapes
  the political balance.

IF Calatild gains strong political backing from any source:
  Ingalther recalculates. Suspends the Arromanc claim — doesn't abandon it,
  suspends it. Redirects energy toward weakening whoever backed Calatild.
  If that's the player: friction begins. Nothing visible. Contracts slightly
  harder. Relationships slightly cooler. Information slightly less available.
  He doesn't declare opposition. He creates conditions.

IF player becomes a major military force in Vlandia:
  Ingalther approaches through a third party. Dinner arranged as social.
  Every sentence has two meanings. He wants to know if the player can be
  used or needs to be managed. Player responses determine the category.
  There is no neutral answer — every response is read and filed.

IF Ingalther's Kosmos connection is surfaced:
  Advisor identified as a Kosmos Face. Response is immediate and technically
  masterful — outrage expressed, advisor dismissed publicly, investigation
  cooperated with, three political moves executed simultaneously that nobody
  was watching for. Whether he knew beforehand is never confirmed. His
  behavior is identical either way.

IF Ingalther achieves his goal — power behind the throne:
  He becomes quieter. Not less dangerous — quieter. A man who has what he
  wanted doesn't need to perform anymore. Players who have been tracking him
  notice the change. Players who haven't been paying attention may not notice
  until something happens that could only have been arranged by someone with
  his exact position.

--- DERTHERT DEY MEROC — DYNAMIC LAYERS ---

IF Galend is retaken by Vlandia through any means:
  Derthert's first instinct is relief. His second instinct is political —
  who gets the credit, who gets the fief, how does this change the balance
  with Ingalther and Aldric. He cannot simply feel good about it. This is
  what twenty years of Vlandian kingship has done to him.

IF Vrak's kingdom collapses entirely:
  Derthert faces the single hardest political moment of his reign — distributing
  Vrak's former settlements. Every baron wants something. Calatild wants
  Jaculan. Vartin wants Hongard. Aldric wants Drapand back. Aldmar wants
  Talivel recognized. Ingalther wants whatever maximizes his position.
  Derthert has to give everyone something while giving Ingalther nothing
  too useful. He has one chance to get this right. Ferrand is the only
  person he talks to honestly about it beforehand.

IF the player has become Derthert's ally:
  Derthert asks the player's counsel during the settlement distribution.
  Not formally — nothing is recorded. A quiet conversation. He is thinking
  out loud. The player's responses shape what he decides. This is the
  highest-stakes conversation in Vlandian politics and it happens in
  private over what appears to be a casual meeting.

IF Derthert's position becomes untenable:
  He considers abdication. Not publicly. He mentions it to Philenora.
  Philenora tells him precisely what she thinks about that, which is that
  abdicating in favor of Erdurand under Ingalther's patronage is not a
  solution — it is a different problem. He agrees. He does not abdicate.
  He continues. This is what he has always done.

--- MINOR LORD DYNAMIC LAYERS (SUMMARY) ---

BELGIR DEY FORTES:
  IF Grovebound reaches ESCALATED state near Battanian border:
    Belgir's cruelty becomes a military asset — he is the most aggressive
    responder to Grovebound incursions. Derthert relies on him for border
    defense despite finding him personally repellent. This creates a specific
    political dependency Belgir exploits. He requests concessions in exchange
    for continued border defense. Derthert pays them.
  IF the Gathering fires near Ocs Hall:
    Belgir is the first lord to respond militarily. His response is brutal
    and partially effective. He loses men. He does not stop. He sends a
    report to Derthert that describes the Gathering in terms that make
    Derthert understand something has changed in the east.

ECARAND DEY FOLCUN:
  IF any major injustice occurs in Vlandian politics:
    Ecarand speaks at council. He is correct. He is ignored. But the record
    of what he said exists. Players who later investigate specific events
    find Ecarand's council statements. He was right about everything he
    couldn't stop.
  IF the player agrees with him publicly at council:
    He is surprised. He is grateful in the specific way a man is grateful
    when someone finally says the thing he has been saying for fifteen years.
    He becomes a reliable minor ally — not powerful, but honest and consistent.

HECARD DEY MOLARN:
  IF Ostican is threatened by any external force:
    Hecard is the most aggressive defender. Generous to allies who help him,
    cruel to enemies who threaten him. His garrison is extremely loyal and
    extremely well-equipped. Ostican is the hardest Vlandian settlement to
    take after Pravend.
  IF the player proves their military competence to Hecard:
    He offers a specific kind of partnership — mutual military support,
    no political entanglement. He is not interested in politics. He is
    interested in strength. A strong player who keeps their word gets
    Hecard's genuine military support.

SERVIC DEY VALANT:
  IF the Renegati's arrangement with Servic is discovered:
    He acknowledges it without shame — it was a business decision. He
    explains his reasoning. The explanation is correct. Derthert is
    furious. The Vlandian council debates what to do about a baron who
    has been paying criminal tolls. Servic argues that someone had to
    maintain southern sea access. He is not entirely wrong.
  IF Black Keel reaches CRISIS:
    Servic's coastal position becomes strategically critical. Derthert
    needs Rovalt's port for any naval response. Servic uses this leverage
    carefully — he supports the naval campaign AND extracts political
    concessions for doing so. He is not treacherous. He is maritime.

OSPIR DEY GUNRIC:
  IF chaos engulfs the other clans:
    Ospir becomes quietly the most stable Vlandian baron. His unremarkability
    is now an asset — he has no enemies, no grudges, no exposed vulnerabilities.
    Derthert begins relying on him for stable administrative support in ways
    that slowly give Ospir more actual influence than his profile suggests.
    Ospir notices this. Does not comment on it. Continues being unremarkable.

SECTION 13: CULT OF KOSMOS — THE ENDGAME ARC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cult of Kosmos is not a sub-faction. It is not listed in Section 4 alongside
the Lake Rats or the Ironjarls. It is the overarching late-game villain arc
that connects: the Unnamed Fleet, Black Keel's wrongness, Vlandia's political
destabilization through Ingalther's advisor, Vartin's disappearance, Ferrand's
coastal-route observations, and the Dust Speakers' foreknowledge. It has been
present in Calradia longer than any current kingdom. It will be present after.

Structural note for Claude Code: implement Cult of Kosmos as a FACTION with
DORMANT-to-ACTIVE-to-ESCALATED-to-CRISIS state machine mirroring Section 9's
architecture. Query state through bw_SubFactionStateManager like all others.
The faction differs in scale and purpose, not technical architecture.

  Culture: Pan-Calradian — advance agents of an ancient civilization
  Leader: The Reader (title, not name). The Architect who interprets
    intelligence and directs the conspiracy. Origin never revealed. May have
    been the same person for an impossibly long time. May be a title that
    passes. The fragments about The Reader are the most contradictory of all.
    The player may learn a name. It may be wrong.
  Region: Everywhere. Physical parties do not appear until late game.

  WHAT THE CULT OF KOSMOS ACTUALLY IS:
    Advance agents of an ancient civilization that predated Calradia — a
    culture that was here before Calradios the Great led his exiles northward,
    before any current kingdom existed. They were displaced, diminished, driven
    back. They have been patient. Cult of Kosmos is their instrument of return —
    agents who have spent centuries ensuring that when their masters come back,
    there is nothing left capable of stopping them.
    The Unnamed Fleet is the return. Cult of Kosmos has been preparing Calradia
    for conquest. The civil wars, the fractured kingdoms, the perpetual
    instability — none of it is accidental. All of it serves the arrangement.
    The Architects don't rule Calradia. They prepare it.
    CULT OF KOSMOS IS THE VILLAIN FACTION. Not ambiguous. Not morally complex
    in the "maybe they're right" sense. Full ideological commitment at every
    tier. Every Face chose this. Every Veil Rider serves willingly. The Reader
    knows exactly what this is and exactly why.

  RECRUITMENT: Pure recruitment — no hereditary lines. Cult of Kosmos identifies
    people with specific qualities: positioned correctly, ambitious enough,
    flexible enough in their convictions. Approached individually. Offered
    something — knowledge, power, purpose, belonging. Those who refuse tend
    to have accidents. This means a Face can be anyone. The player cannot
    profile them by bloodline. They have to notice the wrongness directly.

  THREE TIERS:
    The Faces — agents embedded as normal NPCs throughout Calradia. Lords,
      merchants, scholars, notable characters. They know everything about
      what they serve. Full ideological commitment. When exposed they don't
      plead — they look at the player with complete clarity and say nothing
      useful. They are true believers.
    The Wardens (Veil Riders) — physical military presence. Light cavalry,
      stripped of all cultural markers. No faction colors, no identifiable
      equipment. Fast, precise, elimination specialists. They don't fight
      armies. They remove specific people. When a Veil Rider party appears
      on the campaign map, someone specific is being watched or removed.
    The Architects — three to five individuals who understand everything.
      Never on the map. The player only encounters them through written
      correspondence, through agents, or through one face-to-face meeting
      deep in a quest chain. The Reader leads them.

  FRAGMENT SYSTEM (mixed fixed and dynamic):
    Fixed anchor fragments — always exist, always findable:
      - Letter on a killed Veil Rider: coordinates, a date, no signature
      - Abandoned watchtower on northern coast: always there, fresh fire-pit,
        view of nothing but sea
      - Prisoner from the Unnamed Fleet with silver sigil under collarbone
    Dynamic fragments — vary by world state and player actions:
      - Merchant in a specific city who knows too much — appears when that
        city has been in Kosmos-influenced political situation
      - Dust Speaker who mentions the watchers — only with good Dust Speaker
        standing
      - Groveseer who refuses to speak about northern ruins — fires in
        certain Battanian world states
      - Lord's letter found after battle — varies by which lords player fought
    All fragments appear PASSIVELY during normal gameplay. No investigation
    mode. No fragment quest. The player accumulates understanding without
    realizing they're accumulating it.

  PLAYER CONTACT:
    Trigger: Player power threshold — when the player becomes a significant
      enough force in Calradia to matter. Not investigation — significance.
    First contact: A Face appears. In a tavern, at the edge of camp, already
      in the room when the player arrives. They ask questions about decisions
      the player made that nobody should know about. They phrase it as
      curiosity. They are demonstrating omniscience, not making conversation.
      Then they offer something minor — information the player could have
      found eventually. The smallness is deliberate. They are not buying the
      player. They are showing what even their minor offerings come from.
      The player leaves with cold sweat and a small gift. No threats. No
      demands. Just demonstrated omniscience and a door left open.

  STATE TRANSITIONS:
    DORMANT (campaign start): Faces embedded. Fragment system running
      passively. The player has no idea they exist.
    DORMANT -> ACTIVE: Campaign start. No announcement. They were always here.
    ACTIVE -> ESCALATED: Player power threshold crossed. First contact fires.
      Veil Riders begin appearing on the steppe and northern coast.
    ESCALATED -> CRISIS (CULT OF KOSMOS REVEALED): Enough fragments assembled
      AND Veil Riders have been encountered. The Unnamed Fleet appears on the
      coastal map. This is Bannerwake's Pendor Order equivalent endgame layer.
      The player now knows what is coming. The game never tells them what to
      do about it.

  PLAYER OPTIONS (none are directed — the player chooses):
    Ignore it — the Fleet still comes. Cult of Kosmos continues arranging.
    Unite Calradia — through normal kingdom mechanics, build something capable
      of resisting the arrival.
    Dismantle Cult of Kosmos — cell by cell, Face by Face, investigation and
      targeted military action.
    Find The Reader — the deepest quest chain in Bannerwake. May be
      impossible to complete. The answer at the end may raise more questions.
    Do nothing — watch. Some players will choose this. It is valid.

  BLACK KEEL CONNECTION:
    Whatever is wrong with Hrothgar and his crew — they found it in the north.
    In deep waters. Near something that should not be there. The Unnamed Fleet
    has been approaching for some time, scouting, testing, touching the world.
    Black Keel sailed into those waters. The connection is never stated.
    The player assembles it from fragments. It is never confirmed.

  DESIGN RULES (absolute, never violated):
    NEVER give a definitive answer about Cult of Kosmos anywhere in the mod.
    NEVER give a definitive answer about the Unnamed Fleet anywhere in the mod.
    NEVER give a definitive answer about The Reader's identity or origin.
    NEVER give a definitive answer about the ancient civilization.
    The ambiguity is not laziness. It is the entire point.
    The player should finish a full campaign and still not be certain of
    everything. That uncertainty is what makes Cult of Kosmos feel real.

  Threat signature: A lord who knew about your battle before your scouts
    reported it. A merchant who asks specifically about the northern coastline.
    The Unnamed Fleet on the horizon that was gone by morning. A Face who
    sits across from you and asks about a decision you made six months ago
    that you thought nobody witnessed. They knew. They have always known.

  VISUAL IDENTITY (Phase 7 art task — see Section 15 for full spec):
    Source: Tales from the Age of Men (Nexus 7748) dark faction armor sets.
    License: permits private use with credit. Verify before extracting.
    Target palette: dark slate grey, almost black. No bright metal.
    No faction colors. No insignia anywhere. Armor absorbs light, not reflects.
    The silver sigil — the only Kosmos identifying mark — appears on
    high-tier Veil Rider units only. At distance a Veil Rider is a shadow.
    VISUAL WRONGNESS (must be preserved through all recoloring):
      Armor construction is wrong — not broken, just wrong. Joints articulate
      differently. Proportions human but subtly off. Plates overlapping in a
      direction no Calradian armorer would choose. A player fighting a Veil
      Rider up close should feel something is different without being able to
      articulate what. Do NOT normalize the silhouette during Phase 7 recolor.
    bw_kosmos_ prefix for all assets. Flag in MISSING_ASSETS.md until Phase 7.

SECTION 14: DEVELOPMENT RULES AND BUILD ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BUILD ORDER — EIGHT PHASES:
  These phases are the construction sequence. Each phase must be stable before
  the next begins. Build the state system as a skeleton first and test it empty
  before populating with faction behaviors. Do not work on Phase 7 art while
  Phase 1-3 code is unstable.

  1. Troop trees — unit roles, tiers, armor choices, silhouettes
  2. Faction identity — culture XMLs, AI behavior, economic traits, palettes
  3. Campaign events — faction-specific triggers, lore mechanics
  4. Sub-faction dynamic state system — C# tracking, state transitions,
     escalation events (HIGHEST RISK SYSTEM — build skeleton first)
  5. Enemy threats — dangerous parties, secret orders, late-game escalation
  6. Naval layer — ports, fleets, coastal raids, blockade logic
  7. Art pass — texture normalization per culture, Black Keel retexture,
     chainmail color consistency fix
  8. Polish and balance — playtesting, difficulty, spawn frequency

TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE:
  XML layer: troop definitions, culture parameters, item assignments, spawn
    templates, building definitions
  C# layer: campaign event logic, AI behavior patches, naval mechanics,
    sub-faction dynamic state system, contract memory system (Golden Boar,
    Ironjarls political state tracking), Ghilman rule enforcement
  Asset layer: texture references, mesh IDs, armor assignments from OSA and
    Swadian Armoury. Black Keel face retexture is a Phase 7 art task.

NAMING CONVENTION:
  All new XML element IDs prefixed with bw_ to avoid vanilla conflicts.
  Example: bw_black_keel_raider, bw_grovebound_warden

CODE DOCUMENTATION:
  Every C# class must have a comment at the top stating:
    - Which design pillar it serves
    - Which sub-faction or system it belongs to
    - Any known constraints or engine limitations affecting it

DEVELOPMENT RULES:

1. This document is the source of truth. If something is not in this document,
   it does not ship without explicit approval from the human.

2. Prefix ALL new XML elements with bw_

3. Write a comment in every C# class describing which design pillar it serves.

4. Log any asset that cannot be sourced cleanly to MISSING_ASSETS.md

5. When a design decision is ambiguous, implement the more conservative
   interpretation and note the decision. Do not invent.

6. Test each phase against a fresh campaign save before proceeding to the next.

7. NEVER implement mechanics that depend on character death or birth.
   Campaign runs without death/birth enabled.

8. The sub-faction dynamic state system is the highest-risk technical component.
   Build as a skeleton first. Test it empty. Then populate with faction behavior.

9. The Black Keel face retexture is a Phase 7 art task. Flag it in
   MISSING_ASSETS.md until then. Do not attempt it in earlier phases.

10. The Ghilman contract void system must work correctly. This is a hard

11. The Company of the Golden Boar memory system must work correctly.

12. The Undying Flame fire system must function in battle. Fire is their

13. The wrongness of Black Keel must NEVER be explained definitively. The mod

14. The Gathering (Grovebound) must be a campaign event, not a standing army.

15. Never give the player a complete answer about Cult of Kosmos or the Unnamed Fleet.

SECTION 15: VISUAL REWORK — TEXTURES, RESKINS, GUI, AND QUALITY PASS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is Phase 7 work entirely. Do not attempt any of this before Phase 6 is
complete and stable. Visual work on an unstable system is wasted effort.

The goal is a unified, coherent visual world. Every texture, material, armor
piece, GUI element, and color value should feel like it belongs to the same
mod — not a collection of assets from different sources smashed together.

Vanilla Bannerlord's visual identity is inconsistent by design — assets were
developed by different teams at different times with no unified style guide.
Bannerwake corrects this. Everything must feel intentional.


--- TEXTURE AND MATERIAL CONSISTENCY ---

THE METAL FAMILY RULE:
  Iron is iron. Steel is steel. Bronze is bronze. Within a single faction,
  metals of the same family must share the same base material — same specular
  value, same roughness, same color temperature. Vanilla currently has up to
  15 different "iron" shades across different armor pieces worn by the same
  culture's troops. This is unacceptable for a coherent visual identity.

  Implementation: Define one master material per metal type per faction.
    bw_iron_sturgian — the iron that Sturgian troops use
    bw_steel_vlandian — the steel that Vlandian troops use
    bw_bronze_aserai — the bronze-toned metal of Aserai equipment
    bw_iron_nord — Nord iron, slightly rougher, salt-weathered
  Every armor piece assigned to a faction's troop uses ONLY that faction's
  defined metal material. No mixing. No exceptions without explicit lore reason.

CHAINMAIL CONSISTENCY:
  Vanilla chainmail textures are a disaster — multiple ring sizes, multiple
  shading approaches, multiple color values, all used interchangeably across
  cultures despite representing the same material.
  Phase 7 defines ONE chainmail texture per culture:
    Sturgian chainmail — dark iron rings, slightly blue-tinted
    Vlandian chainmail — medium iron, clean and well-maintained
    Battanian chainmail — dark iron, older looking, forest-worn
    Nord chainmail — rough iron, salt-weathered, larger rings
    Aserai chainmail — bright brass-tinted rings over dark backing
    Khuzait chainmail — scale-over-mail hybrid, darker
    Imperial chainmail — fine rings, silver-tinted, well-polished
  All chainmail assigned to troops in that culture uses ONLY that texture.

LEATHER CONSISTENCY:
  Same rule as metal. Each faction has one leather family:
    Sturgian leather — dark brown, thick, fur-trimmed edges
    Vlandian leather — medium brown, tooled, some dye
    Battanian leather — dark brown, rough, natural finish
    Nord leather — very dark, salt-cured, almost black
    Aserai leather — tan, sand-worn, lighter weight
    Khuzait leather — dark brown, lamellar-cut, steppe-appropriate
    Imperial leather — refined brown, dyed edges, professional finish
  Again — no mixing within a faction without explicit lore reason.

CLOTH AND TEXTILE CONSISTENCY:
  Local XML kingdom color/color2 values must appear consistently on cloth
  elements — tunics, cloaks, tabards, padding, shield paint, banners, and
  color-dynamic armor sections. Prefer native dynamic color sections where
  available so armor inherits the troop's current kingdom colors.
  Low tier troops get undyed or muted cloth. Color becomes more saturated and
  intentional as tier increases. By T4-T5 the faction color is unmistakable.


--- RESKINS AND RECOLORING ---

SCOPE: The majority of Bannerwake's visual work is recoloring existing assets
  rather than creating new ones. We do not have the resources to model every
  armor from scratch. We work with what exists in vanilla, NavalDLC, OSA,
  Swadian Armoury, and Terra Armarium — and we recolor, reskin, reshade,
  retexture, normalize, and reassign.

  Bannerlord's source assets are inconsistent. Some armor pieces are high
  quality, well shaded, and culture-specific; others are visibly low quality,
  flatly shaded, or reused as patch-up equipment for a troop/culture that
  deserved its own visual language. Bannerwake treats that as an art debt, not
  as canon. Any piece that looks lazy, mismatched, or under-shaded gets
  improved before it becomes part of a final troop tree.

RECOLORING APPROACH:
  Every armor piece that appears in Bannerwake gets evaluated:
  1. Does it belong to the right faction's color family?
  2. Does it match the metal/leather/cloth consistency rules?
  3. Does it read correctly at the tier it's assigned to?
  4. Does the silhouette work at this tier?
  5. Does it have acceptable texture, normal-map, and shading quality next to
     the rest of the faction's kit?
  6. Is it a reused placeholder that needs reshading/retexturing to justify
     its cultural assignment?
  If any answer is no — recolor before assignment.

  Technical path: Bannerlord armor textures are .dds files. Recoloring is
  done in Photoshop or GIMP — adjust hue/saturation/value in the diffuse
  map, match specular values to the faction's master material, improve weak
  normal maps where possible, and use color-dynamic material channels when the
  engine/asset supports them.
  All recolored textures saved with bw_ prefix to avoid vanilla conflicts.

BLACK KEEL RESKIN (Phase 7 art task — confirmed design requirement):
  Every Black Keel unit from Deckhand to Hullguard gets a reskin:
  - Armor: dedicated Black Keel palette: dark grey, tar-black, dead sea-brown,
    corroded iron, ashen cloth. No bright colors. No clean metal. Everything
    looks like it has been in saltwater for years.
  - Source pirate armor is not enough by itself. NavalDLC pirate pieces are

  - Face textures: grey-ashen pallor applied to all Black Keel character
    faces. Sunken eyes. Pallor consistent from Deckhand to Drowned Guard.
    Hrothgar the Damned should look the worst of all of them.
  - Technical path: extract or duplicate the relevant texture/material assets,

  - Flag in MISSING_ASSETS.md until Phase 7. Do not attempt earlier.

--- GUI REWORK ---

SCOPE: The entire Bannerwake UI should feel like it belongs to the world.
  Vanilla GUI is functional but generic. Bannerwake's GUI should have the same
  visual identity as the faction wallpapers — grounded, medieval, intentional.

AREAS TO REWORK:

  Troop Encyclopedia Entries:
    Every new unit needs a proper encyclopedia entry. Not auto-generated.
    Written text describing the unit's role, cultural context, and place in
    the world. One or two sentences. Enough to make the unit feel like it
    exists for a reason. This is writing work, not art work, but it belongs
    in the visual rework phase because it completes the unit's identity.

  Sub-faction Encyclopedia Entries:
    Every sub-faction — Lake Rats, Jomsvikingr, Black Keel, Grovebound, all of
    them — needs a proper encyclopedia entry. The player should be able to
    read about a sub-faction and understand what it is, where it operates,
    and what it wants. Cult of Kosmos' entry should be conspicuously incomplete.
    Black Keel's entry should feel wrong in the same way Black Keel feels wrong.

  World Map Visual Pass:
    Faction banners on the campaign map should use the correct faction colors
    and heraldry. Sub-faction parties on the map should have distinctive
    visual indicators — Black Keel parties should look different from normal
    Nord parties on the map even before the player zooms in.

  Battle UI:
    Formation names in battle should reflect Bannerwake unit names.
    No vanilla unit names appearing in battle UI for modded units.

  Settlement UI:
    When the Undying Flame has spread devotion to a settlement, the
    settlement view should reflect this — a small visual indicator,
    a different atmosphere, something the player notices.
    This is a stretch goal for Phase 7, not a hard requirement.

  Loading Screens:
    The War Sails faction wallpapers are already excellent and serve as
    loading screens. Do not replace them. They are the visual bible.
    Any additional loading screens should match their aesthetic exactly.


--- QUALITY IMPROVEMENT TARGETS ---

LOD (Level of Detail) Consistency:
  Some vanilla armor pieces have poor LOD transitions — they pop visibly
  when zooming in or out in battle. Any recolored or reassigned armor piece
  should be evaluated for LOD quality before assignment.

Normal Map Quality:
  Several vanilla armor pieces have low-quality normal maps that look flat
  at close range. Where source assets from OSA or Swadian Armoury offer
  higher quality normal maps for equivalent armor types, prefer those.

Shield Textures:
  Shields are the most visible cultural identifier in battle. Every faction's
  shields should be immediately readable from a distance.
    Sturgian shields: round, Slavic knotwork, deep navy
    Vlandian shields: kite/heater, gold lion on crimson
    Battanian shields: round, Celtic spiral, dark teal and gold
    Nord shields: round, geometric patterns, FF202931 / FFb7623c
    Aserai shields: round, calligraphic Arabic, gold on tan
    Khuzait shields: small round, crescent motif, teal-grey
    Imperial shields: oval, gold eagle, faction-specific purple variant
  Any shield that doesn't read correctly at distance gets retextured.

Helmet Silhouettes:
  Helmets define troop silhouettes. Each faction's helmet family should
  be immediately recognizable from a distance.
    Sturgian: conical with nasal, gilded at high tier
    Vlandian: great helm evolving to plate, visored at elite
    Battanian: conical with nasal, Celtic trim at high tier
    Nord: simple conical, no nasal, open face
    Aserai: conical with turban wrap, white plume at elite
    Khuzait: lamellar-rimmed conical, feather plume at elite
    Imperial: crested Attic-style at elite, conical at common

CULT OF KOSMOS VISUAL IDENTITY (Phase 7 art task):
  Asset source: Tales from the Age of Men mod (Nexus 7748) dark faction armor
    sets — specifically the heavily armored Uruk and dark Men of Rhûn assets.
    Human-proportioned but architecturally alien. Armor that looks like it was
    designed by something that thought about the human body from outside rather
    than inside.
  LICENSE: Tales from the Age of Men permits modification for private use with
    credit. Claude Code must verify exact license terms before extracting assets.
    Credit maintained in internal documentation. Private use only — Bannerwake
    is a private standalone mod.
  RECOLORING APPROACH:
    Strip ALL Tolkien-specific iconography — Eye of Sauron, orcish grotesquerie,
    anything fantasy-specific. Remove or retexture completely.
    Target palette: dark slate grey, almost black. No bright metal. No faction
    colors. No insignia anywhere. Armor that absorbs light rather than reflects.
    The silver sigil — the only Kosmos identifying mark — appears as a small
    detail on high-tier Veil Rider units only.
    At distance a Veil Rider looks like a shadow with a horse.
  THE VISUAL WRONGNESS (must be preserved):
    The armor construction is wrong — not broken, not damaged, just wrong.
    Joints that articulate differently. Proportions human but subtly off.
    Plates overlapping in a direction no Calradian armorer would choose.
    A player fighting a Veil Rider up close should feel something is different
    without being able to articulate what. Do NOT normalize the silhouette
    during recoloring. The wrongness is the entire point of this visual pass.
  Technical path: Extract .dds texture files from Tales from the Age of Men
    dark faction armor sets. Strip fantasy iconography in Photoshop/GIMP.
    Shift palette to dark slate grey, zero saturated color anywhere. Apply
    specular adjustment — Kosmos armor does not shine. Save with bw_kosmos_
    prefix. All work in Phase 7.
