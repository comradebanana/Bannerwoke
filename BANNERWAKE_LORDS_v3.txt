  CURT       — Clipped. Efficient. Every word chosen. Silence is also a tool.
               Does not elaborate unless it serves a purpose.
  EARNEST    — Means what they say. Direct. Warmth, when present, is real.

STURGIA OVERVIEW:
  The tension of Sturgia is simple on the surface and irresolvable underneath:
  Raganvad believes the prince commands the boyars in all things. The boyars —
  especially Kuloving — believe the prince commands in war and that's as far
  as the oath goes. Neither position is wrong on its own terms. The gap between
  them has been managed for twenty years. It has not been closed.
  The Pendraic battle is the wound that won't scar. Olek the Old led from the
  front and died. Raganvad gave orders from behind the line and survived. Every
  boyar who thinks the prince should lead from the front has never forgiven this.
  Raganvad has never apologized for it. He followed his father's orders. That
  is what he did. The boyars can think what they like.
  Ov Castle [castle_S5] is the chokepoint. Whoever holds Ov Castle controls
  the most strategically significant position in Sturgia. Vashorki holds it.
  He is generally considered despicable. He continues to hold it.

--- RAGANVAD [lord_2_1] — GRAND PRINCE OF STURGIA ---
  voice CURT / age 37 / male
  Traits: Authoritarian, Calculating, Commander 14, Closefisted, Devious,
    HuscarlFightingSkills 6, Manager 12, Cruel, Politician 16, Daring
  Family: spouse Asta [lord_2_2]; children Valla [lord_2_10], Simir [lord_2_7],
    Mimir [lord_2_7_1]
  XML note: "Ragenvad, Hardrada type." "Genuine monarchist."
  Lore: "The current Grand Prince of Sturgia is Raganvad. Harsh and
    uncompromising, he believes that it is the right of the prince to command
    the boyars in all things, not just in making war. For the time being they
    obey him..."
  Political philosophy (verbatim XML strings):
    "The boyars swore an oath to my grandfather to obey him as prince. They
    begged him to save them from the wrath of the Empire, which they had stirred
    up with their raids and their lawless ways."
    "My grandfather did save the boyars, and ever since, their thankless heirs
    have tried to weasel out of that oath. They like to quarrel, and take what
    they want, and care nothing for the realm."
    "I will break them. By the Heavens I will bring them to heel, or the knife
    will fall on them."

  PRIVATE LIFE:
    Raganvad reads correspondence before dawn. Not because his day is too busy
    to read it later — because he prefers to know what is happening before he
    is required to respond to it. Asta knows this and leaves him alone in the
    first hour. The household knows not to disturb the first hour. He has
    calculated exactly how much force it would take to eliminate each boyar
    house in sequence without triggering a coalition against him. He has been
    refining this calculation for years. He has never shared it with anyone.
    He does not intend to use it. He intends to not have to use it.

  DIALOGUE:
    SCENE: Varcheg's great hall, or his campaign tent in the field. He is always
      mid-task when the player arrives — a document, a report, a calculation.
      He finishes the sentence. He does not look up until he is done. This is
      not rudeness. It is accurate prioritization. Then he looks at the player
      with the complete attention of someone who has just decided they matter.

    FIRST ENCOUNTER:
      "Raganvad." His own name, nothing else. He does not offer a title in
      first encounter. You either know who he is or you do not.
      "What do you want?" Two words. How the player states their want tells him
      more than the want itself.
      IF player states a clear practical need: He acknowledges it. No warmth
        performed. "I'll consider it. Come back tomorrow."
      IF player offers flattery: He waits for it to end. "What do you want?"
        He has heard flattery. It provides no information.
      IF player mentions the boyars: He looks at them differently. Not with
        hostility — with assessment. Someone who has come in and mentioned the
        boyars in a first meeting is either useful or dangerous or both.
        "Sit." He wants to hear the rest.

    LOW:
      Minimum accurate words. The irony of a man who says the minimum is that
      every word he chooses is the exact right word.
      On Pendraic: "I followed orders." He says it flatly. He has said this
        many times. He believes it. He also knows it is the answer that ends
        this line of conversation, which is why he uses it.
      On the boyars: "They have opinions. Some of them are not entirely wrong."
        He will not say which ones or how far they extend.
      On Varra's death (Olek's sister): "A tragedy." One word. Nothing more.
        The player who watches his face when he says it learns nothing from it.

    MID:
      He has decided the player is worth some of his time. This shows as
      slightly longer sentences, not as warmth.
      On Olek: "He has opinions. They are not wrong. They are incomplete."
        He will not say what is incomplete. He has thought about what is
        incomplete. He intends to demonstrate it, not explain it.
      On Simir: "He is cold. He will be effective." The flatness of a man who
        has decided this is sufficient and has not entirely decided it.
      On Asta: "She knows everything I know, eventually." He says this
        without elaboration. The player is expected to understand the word
        "eventually."
      On the political philosophy question (if player asks how he sees his
        own authority): He looks at them for a moment. Then he recites the
        political philosophy verbatim — not from memory but from the specific
        conviction of a man who has been saying this for years.
        "My grandfather saved the boyars. They swore an oath. I intend to
        hold them to it." He says it simply. He means every word.

    HIGH:
      He says one thing he has never said in public. Once. He does not repeat
      it and the player cannot ask him to repeat it.
      On Pendraic, once:
        "My father gave me an order. I followed it." Long pause.
        "What they think of that is their business." The flatness is not
        indifference. It is a man who has made peace with something that
        cannot be unmade. The player who responds with "you were right to
        follow it" gets nothing further. The player who stays silent gets:
        "Yes. I was." He believes this. He has had to believe this.
      On Simir, once, at the highest relationship:
        "He was cold as a child. I watched this and decided it was useful."
        He looks at the player. "I have not entirely decided it was enough."
        He has never said this to Asta. The player is the first person outside
        his own head who has heard it.

    NEVER:
      Shows warmth directly. Admits his deviousness as a category. Discusses
      his Closefistedness. Blames a specific boyar by name in a way that could
      be repeated. Apologizes for Pendraic. Expresses doubt about his own
      authority in any public setting. Uses more words than the minimum.
      Threatens without intending to follow through.

--- OLEK [lord_2_3] ---
  voice CURT / age 48 / male
  Traits: Generous, Cruel, Oligarchic, Daring, Commander 12,
    HuscarlFightingSkills 8, Manager 6, Politician 10
  Family: father Olek the Old [dead_lord_2_1]; children Urik [lord_2_8],
    Idrun [lord_2_11], Rozhivol [lord_2_111]; siblings Varra [dead_lord_2_2],
    Siga [lord_2_4]
  Lore: "Olek is patriarch of the Kuloving, one of the oldest of the Sturgian
    clans, which never really accepted the idea of the monarchy. He believes
    the boyars should be lords in their own lands. He is famed for once telling
    [Raganvad] that every wound he suffered in battle under his banner was like
    his wife's caress, but every denar he paid in tax was like an arrow in
    his gut."
  Political philosophy (verbatim XML string):
    "Our family have been the masters of this corner of the woods since the
    gods willed that the trees grow and the rivers flow. Someone wants to call
    himself Prince of the Sturgians? We have no quarrel with that. Just let
    that prince know that on our land, we're still the masters."
  NOTE: Called OLEK in-game, not "Olek the Young." Remove all instances of
    "Olek the Young" from all documents.

  PRIVATE LIFE:
    He carves. Not as a hobby — he carves the same kind of work his father
    carved, and his grandfather, and further back. Small wooden figures of the
    old gods that technically aren't worshipped anymore and practically are.
    He places them at the edges of his hall without announcement. He doesn't
    talk about them. The boyars who visit know what they mean. The boyars who
    don't know, don't matter. He also still doesn't know what happened to Varra.
    He has suspects. He has no proof. He has been carrying both for twenty years.

  DIALOGUE:
    SCENE: Omor's hall. Olek is usually standing — not pacing, standing,
      the way a man stands who has spent more time in the field than at a table.
      He looks at the player with the directness of someone who has already
      decided what they are before hearing them speak. He can be wrong about
      this. He adjusts without making a point of it.

    FIRST ENCOUNTER:
      "Olek. Kuloving." His name and his clan. That is his introduction.
      He waits. He wants the player to state their purpose.
      IF player states business directly: He engages immediately.
        "Good. That I can work with."
      IF player opens with deference or formality: He cuts it.
        "What do you need?" He is not rude. He is efficient.
      IF player mentions Raganvad first: He gives them a long look.
        "Sent by the prince?" He waits. The player's answer changes
        the register of the entire conversation.

    LOW:
      On the oath to Raganvad: He states his political philosophy directly.
        "The Kuloving have never accepted the monarchy. We accept it now
        because the alternative is worse. This is not endorsement." Every word
        is exactly what he means.
      On Pendraic: "My father died there. He led from the front."
        He does not say Raganvad's name. He does not need to. He does not
        embellish. That is the complete statement.
      On taxation: "Every denar I pay in tax is an arrow in my gut."
        He says it the same way the lore records it. He has said it many times.
        He means it every time.

    MID:
      On Varra: He mentions her once. Flat and specific.
        "My sister died the year after Pendraic. Not in battle. Under
        circumstances that have not been explained to my satisfaction."
        He pauses. "I have suspicions. I have no proof." He moves on.
        He has made peace with carrying it. He has not made peace with
        not knowing.
      On Urik: "Aggressive and deceitful. More than I was." He says this
        with the same flatness he uses for everything else. This is a
        compliment in the Kuloving's terms and an accurate description.
      On the Vagiroving: "Godun cooperates with Raganvad because it's
        convenient. He calls it wisdom. It is convenient." He says this
        without visible malice. He simply does not call it something it isn't.

    HIGH:
      On Varra, once, at the highest relationship:
        "I know who I think it was. I have never said the name out loud."
        He looks at the player. "I am not going to say it now." He means
        this. He is not going to say it now. The player cannot know whose
        name he is not saying. The player may later find out through other
        means. Olek will not be the source.
      If the player has high Honor: "You've kept your word twice. That's
        more than most." This is the highest praise he gives. It is two
        sentences. It is sincere.

    NEVER:
      Uses more words than are necessary. Performs the Kuloving's resistance
      as theater — it is his genuine position, not an act. Forgives Pendraic.
      Admits warmth that isn't there. Pretends to be satisfied with the oath
      he's sworn.

THREAD A: THE PENDRAIC WOUND
The battle, the death, the unanswered year after.
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
  The Battle of Pendraic is Sturgia's open wound. Three connected events:
    1. Raganvad's father ordered him to hold the reserve. He did.
       Olek the Old led from the front. He died.
    2. Raganvad survived. The boyars remember who commanded them from behind
       the line.
    3. Varra — Olek's sister — died the year after Pendraic. Under mysterious
       circumstances. Not in battle. The circumstances have never been explained
       to the Kuloving's satisfaction.

  These three events are connected in the boyars' memory even if Varra's
  death was completely unrelated to the battle. Olek has suspicions. Siga
  has suspicions. The player can investigate. The document does not confirm
  what the investigation finds.

DISCOVERY:
  LAYER 1 — Available to any player who speaks to Olek at low relationship:
    "My father died at Pendraic. He led from the front." He does not say
    more. The player now has the data point.

  LAYER 2 — Available when player speaks to both Olek and Raganvad at mid:
    Raganvad: "I followed orders." (his answer to any Pendraic question)
    Olek: "I know what he says. I know what I saw." He does not elaborate.
    The contradiction is in the gap between these two responses. The player
    has now seen both sides. Neither man is entirely wrong.

  LAYER 3 — Siga at mid relationship, Varra thread:
    "My sister died the year after Pendraic. Not in battle. Under circumstances
    that were not explained to my satisfaction." She pauses. "I have carried
    both my suspicions and my inability to act on them for a long time."
    The player now knows Varra's death is a separate unresolved question.

  LAYER 4 — Olek at HIGH relationship:
    "I know who I think it was. I have never said the name out loud. I am not
    going to say it now." He means this. He will not say it.
    The player can try to investigate through other means.

INVESTIGATION (if player pursues it):
  In a specific Sturgian settlement, a specific older merchant knows something
  about the year after Pendraic. What they know is partial. What it implies
  is specific but deniable. The player can accumulate enough fragments to form
  a conclusion. The document does not state what the correct conclusion is.
  DESIGN RULE: Never confirm the cause of Varra's death. The ambiguity
    is intentional. The conclusion the player reaches is their conclusion.
    Different players will reach different conclusions from the same fragments.
    All of them are plausible. None of them are confirmed.

================================================================================
THREAD B: OV CASTLE
Everyone needs it. Vashorki holds it. Everyone deals with him.
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
  Ov Castle [castle_S5] is the most strategically significant position in
  Sturgia. Controlling it means controlling movement through the central
  corridor. Raganvad needs it loyal. Olek needs it not loyal to Raganvad.
  The Vagiroving's eastern trade ambitions require it accessible. Vashorki
  holds it and is generally held to be despicable.

  The thread is not about taking Ov Castle. It is about what it costs to
  maintain a relationship with the man who holds it, and what it reveals
  about every other lord who has already made that calculation.

DISCOVERY:
  LAYER 1 — Player visits Ov Castle, meets Vashorki. He is exactly what
    the description says. He announces this himself. "You've heard of me,
    I expect."

  LAYER 2 — Player speaks to Raganvad at mid about Vashorki. Raganvad
    does not say Vashorki's name. He says: "Ov Castle is held by someone
    whose reliability I manage rather than depend on." One sentence. He
    does not elaborate. His face does not change.

  LAYER 3 — Player speaks to Olek at mid about Vashorki. Olek:
    "He is despicable. He holds the castle. These are both facts."
    He does not add anything. He has thought about this. His conclusion
    is that both facts coexist and he manages his relationship with both.

  LAYER 4 — Player speaks to Godun about Vashorki:
    "An unusual man in a strategically important position. I have found
    that dealing with him directly is more efficient than dealing with him
    through intermediaries, which is what everyone else does." He is not
    endorsing Vashorki. He is describing his own calculation.

  LAYER 5 — Ratagost on Vashorki (mid relationship):
    "He is what he is. The castle needs someone who will hold it without
    apology. He holds it without apology. This may be sufficient."

PLAYER OPTIONS:
  A. Build relationship with Vashorki directly. He becomes a direct
     intelligence source. What he knows about Sturgian politics from
     his position is substantial and accurate. The cost is that everyone
     who knows the player deals with Vashorki adjusts their assessment
     of the player slightly.
  B. Try to find leverage over Vashorki. He has anticipated this. He
     finds it more interesting than threatening. He offers to sell the
     information the player found.
  C. Report to Raganvad that a specific something is wrong with Vashorki's
     arrangements. Raganvad files it. He does not act on it immediately.
     Three campaigns later, Vashorki's position becomes more precarious in
     a way that started with this conversation.
  D. Leave Vashorki alone. The castle continues operating. He continues
     being despicable. The political situation continues being managed.
     This is probably what most players do. It is a valid choice.
