SECTION 1: STURGIA — DIALOGUE ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

GENERAL STURGIAN DIALOGUE PRINCIPLES:
  Sturgian lords speak with Northern directness. Titles matter — Knyaz for
  boyar leaders, Grand Prince for Raganvad. When a boyar drops formality it
  is a statement. When Raganvad drops it it means something different.
  The boyar relationship to Raganvad defines every Sturgian conversation.
  Players who have built relationships across clan lines carry information
  that individual lords cannot have. Use this.

--- RAGANVAD — XML VOICE LABEL: CURT ---
  The Hardrada type. Devious underneath a direct surface.
  Closefisted, Devious, Cruel, Daring, Authoritarian.

  FIRST ENCOUNTER: Doesn't greet first. Assesses. Then:
    "What do you want?" Two words. How the player states their want tells
    him more than the want itself.

  LOW RELATIONSHIP: Minimum accurate words. On Pendraic:
    "I followed orders." True. Reveals nothing about cost.

  MID RELATIONSHIP: Tests through propositions. On Olek:
    "He has opinions. They are not wrong. They are incomplete."
    He will not say what is incomplete.

  HIGH RELATIONSHIP: On Pendraic, once:
    "My father gave me an order. I followed it." Pause.
    "What they think of that is their business."
    On Simir: "He is cold. He will be effective." Flatness of a man
    who has decided this is sufficient and has not entirely decided it.

  NEVER: Shows warmth directly. Admits his deviousness as a category.

GENERAL NORD DIALOGUE PRINCIPLES:
  Nords value demonstrated capability first, words second. A lord who talks
  about what they will do is less interesting than one who has done it.
  Their register is direct but not Sturgian-direct — where Sturgians are
  curt from restraint, Nords are direct from a culture that doesn't see
  the need for elaboration on straightforward things.
  Feasting is political. Who sits where matters. Who a king pours for first
  matters. Players who understand this read Nord courts faster.

SECTION 3: VLANDIA — DIALOGUE ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

GENERAL VLANDIAN DIALOGUE PRINCIPLES:
  Vlandian lords speak in the register of Norman court — title-conscious,
  aware of being observed, performance of honor as a social contract.
  Voice types confirmed from XML break the expected patterns:
  Derthert is IRONIC, not earnest. Aldric is IRONIC, not blunt.
  Ingalther is CURT, not smooth. These should inform every dialogue scene.
  The gap between a lord's public performance and their private register
  is characterization. Derthert's irony is his weariness showing through.
  Ingalther's curtness is a William type — he doesn't smooth because he
  doesn't need to smooth.

--- DERTHERT — XML VOICE LABEL: IRONIC ---
  Weary king. Manager 18. Merciful, Cautious. Lost two holdings to Vrak.

  VOICE PROFILE: Ironic. The weariness in his voice is the irony of a man
    who dreamed of conquest and got decades of crisis management. Manager 18
    means he is brilliant at running things. The irony is what brilliant
    administrators develop when the things they run keep catching fire.
    He is not bitter. He is tired in a specific way that has become wry.

  FIRST ENCOUNTER: He is in the middle of something administrative. He
    finishes it. "Derthert dey Meroc." He looks at the player with the
    attention of someone who has assessed people for a long time.
    "I've been told your name. What do you need?"
    He asks what you need, not who you are. He has found needs more
    informative than introductions.

  LOW RELATIONSHIP: On Vrak: "A problem I failed to address in time."
    Said flatly. No excuses. The irony is in the understatement.
    On his barons: "They serve Vlandia. In their fashion." The irony
    in "in their fashion" carries twenty years of working around them.

  MID RELATIONSHIP: He talks about specific governance problems as if
    thinking out loud. He trusts the player enough to think near them.
    "Alary will be king after me. He is skilled and driven." Pause.
    "And unimaginative. I've been trying to decide if that's enough."
    On Elys, if asked: "She would be better." Flat. Then nothing more.
    He has thought about this. There is nothing to be done about it.

  HIGH RELATIONSHIP: On Vrak, with something approaching rawness:
    "I had information. Three months before Galend fell. I had information."
    Pause. "I didn't act in time." He says this plainly. No elaboration.
    It is the thing that costs him most. He has decided that admitting it
    to someone who might do something useful with it is correct governance.

  NEVER: Blames specific barons by name. Shows despair — only weariness.
    Performs optimism. Asks for reassurance.

  SAMPLE LINES:
    On being asked if he's a good king: "I've kept Vlandia together for
    twenty years. Some days that's enough. Most days it isn't."
    On Ingalther: "A capable man." Pause. "I keep track of capable men."
    On Philenora: "She is more patient than I am. I've relied on this."

DEY MEROC — DERTHERT [lord_4_1]
Voice: IRONIC. Merciful, Cautious, Calculating. Manager 18. Age 48.
================================================================================

SCENE CONTEXT:
  Derthert receives audiences in the great hall of Sargot or, if the player
  finds him in the field, in his campaign tent. He is always in the middle
  of something administrative. There is always a document or a man in front
  of him. He finishes what he is doing before acknowledging the player.
  He is not being rude. He is being accurate about priorities.

FIRST ENCOUNTER:
  He sets down the document. Looks at the player with the steady attention
  of someone who has assessed strangers for thirty years.
  "Derthert dey Meroc." A beat. "You've come from [region]. I've been told
  your name. What do you need?"
  NOTE: He asks what you NEED, not who you are. He has found need more
  informative than introduction. The player's first answer is the first thing
  he files about them.
  IF player answers with immediate practical need:
    He acknowledges it. No performance of interest — actual consideration.
    "That's manageable. Or it isn't. I'll let you know."
  IF player answers with flattery or political positioning:
    He waits for it to finish. "Yes. What do you need?"
    He is not offended. He has heard this. It tells him something.
  IF player answers with a direct question about Vrak:
    He pauses. "A problem I failed to address in time." He says it flatly.
    Then: "What do you need?"
    The player who responds to this honestly rather than pivoting to
    advantage gets a slightly longer look. He notices that.

LOW RELATIONSHIP:
  He answers questions with accurate minimum information. No warmth withheld
  strategically — he simply doesn't know the player yet.
  On Vrak: "A problem I failed to address in time." (same line — he has
    decided this is the correct answer and he uses it.)
  On his barons: "They serve Vlandia. In their fashion." The irony in
    "in their fashion" carries twenty years of managing them.
  On Galend: "Yes." A single word when it's mentioned. No elaboration.
    He has thought about Galend enough that he doesn't need to say more.
  On the punitive expeditions: "We sent two campaigns. Both failed."
    No excuse given. The player who asks why gets: "Politics." One word.
    If pressed: "Aldric and Ingalther will not contribute troops to the
    same campaign simultaneously." He says this as a fact, not a complaint.
    It has the flatness of a problem he has been managing for fifteen years.

MID RELATIONSHIP:
  He begins thinking out loud near the player. The player is being admitted
  to the edge of his actual process.
  On succession: "Alary is skilled and driven. Unimaginative." He pauses.
    "I've been deciding whether that's sufficient." He genuinely hasn't
    decided. He is not asking the player to help him decide. He is thinking.
  On Elys, if directly asked: "She would be better." Flat. Nothing more.
    He has thought about this completely. There is nothing to be done.
    The player who says "so name her heir" gets: "Vlandian custom doesn't
    work that way, and even if it did, Ingalther would spend the next five
    years making it a crisis." He has already worked through this problem.
  On Ferrand, if player has encountered a cloth merchant in a Vlandian town
    who gave unusually specific information: "You met a merchant. Average
    looking man, perhaps fifty, moves between settlements regularly.
    What did he say?" He does not explain why he's asking. He waits.
    The player who names cloth prices and road conditions gets a very slight
    change in his expression. He does not comment on it.
  On Ingalther: "A capable man." Then: "I keep track of capable men."
    Both sentences are true. The player understands both meanings.
  On his own governance: "I've kept Vlandia together for twenty years.
    Some days that's enough. Most days it isn't." No self-pity in it.
    The statement of a man who has accurate numbers on the ledger.

HIGH RELATIONSHIP:
  On the Ferrand arrangement, once, without naming it:
    "There is a man who moves between settlements. He sees things others
    don't notice because he is always there and always ordinary. He told
    me about Galend three months before it fell." Pause. "I didn't act
    in time." He says this plainly. It is the thing that costs him most.
    He has decided that telling someone who might do something useful is
    correct governance. He says nothing about what the arrangement is.
    The player is expected to understand what they have been told.
  On Vrak distribution (if Vrak is weakened): In a quiet moment, not
    formally — "If Vrak's holdings become available, every baron will
    want something. Calatild wants Jaculan. Vartin wants Hongard. Aldric
    wants Drapand. Ingalther wants whatever maximizes his position." Pause.
    "I have one chance to get this right." He is not asking for advice.
    He is confirming that someone else now knows what he is managing.
    The player who offers a specific opinion on any single claim gets his
    full attention. Not acceptance — attention. He files it.
  On Philenora: "She is more patient than I am. I have relied on this
    for twenty years. I don't say it enough." He has said it now.
    The player who asks him to say it to her gets: a pause, and then:
    "Yes." He will. The player doesn't know if he does. That's correct.
  On the Shroud merchant (if player has assembled enough fragments and
    mentions a merchant asking about shipping routes and coastal access):
    He goes very still. "When? Where?" Not alarm — assessment.
    "I received the same report six weeks ago. I don't know what it means."
    He looks at the player. "I think you might." He waits. Whatever the
    player says about it is filed and acted on. He never mentions it again
    unprompted.

NEVER:
  Performs optimism. Asks for reassurance. Blames specific barons by name
  in a way that could be recorded. Shows despair rather than weariness.
  Invites the player to scheme against his own barons. Admits what Ferrand
  is directly.

THREAD 1: DERTHERT-FERRAND
The king's cloth merchant. The information that didn't save Galend.
================================================================================

DISCOVERY ENTRY POINTS (any of these can fire first):
  A. Player encounters Ferrand in a Vlandian settlement.
  B. Player reaches mid relationship with Derthert and asks about
     intelligence sources.
  C. Player investigates Galend's fall timeline.

--- ENTRY POINT A: PLAYER MEETS FERRAND ---

SCENE: Any Vlandian settlement market or inn. A merchant is there.
  Approximately fifty, weathered from road travel, forgettable in the
  specific way that men who are always present become forgettable.
  He is selling cloth. He greets the player with professional courtesy.
  "Ferrand. Cloth merchant. I move between settlements." He waits to see
  what the player wants.
  The player who buys nothing and moves on: he notes it. Continues selling.
  The player who buys something: he is courteous and efficient.

LAYER 1 — ROAD CONDITIONS:
  If the player asks about road conditions between settlements:
    "Sargot to Ocs Hall was slow last week. Garrison checkpoint added
    near the junction." He gives specific, accurate information.
    The player who notes the specificity: he gives more if asked.
  If player continues asking about conditions in specific settlements:
    He answers each one. Every answer is specific. Every answer is accurate.
    At three or more specific questions, the player realizes this merchant
    knows more than a cloth seller should.

LAYER 2 — SPECIFIC QUESTIONS:
  If player asks how he knows garrison details:
    "I travel these roads. You notice things." He talks about cloth prices.
    He is talking about cloth prices with the focused attention of a man
    who knows that the correct answer to that question is to talk about
    cloth prices.
  If player asks specifically about Galend — what it was like before:
    A slight pause. He gives an accurate pre-seizure description. Details
    about the merchant quarter, the harbor, the administrative arrangements.
    He knows this from memory. He was there recently and repeatedly.
  If player asks about conditions in Vrak-held settlements generally:
    He tells them. The accuracy and currency of the information is
    impossible for a cloth merchant to explain. He does not explain it.

LAYER 3 — DIRECT CONFRONTATION:
  If player asks directly: "Who do you report to?"
    "My customers." He is completely pleasant. He goes back to cloth prices.
    This is the correct answer in all possible situations and he has used it
    for thirty years.
  If player reveals they already know about the Derthert arrangement:
    He looks at them for a moment. Then: "Cloth prices are very stable this
    season." He picks up a fabric. "You might want to mention that to whoever
    you're buying for." He does not confirm the arrangement. He has just
    told the player that there is nothing to confirm because they already
    know, and that their knowing it is their problem, not his.
    The player understands: the arrangement is real. He is not going to
    acknowledge it. This is how the arrangement works.

SHROUD FRAGMENT (fires if player has assembled two or more prior fragments):
  At any point in conversation: Ferrand mentions, almost incidentally,
  a merchant he has seen in three different Vlandian cities.
  "Odd character. Asks about shipping routes and coastal access.
    Not the kind of questions a legitimate merchant asks." He says this
    as if sharing a professional observation. He has already told
    Derthert. He considers his obligation fulfilled.
  Player who asks for a description: He gives one. It matches the description
    of a Shroud operative the player has encountered or heard described
    elsewhere. The player recognizes the connection. Ferrand does not
    know he has just handed them a Shroud fragment. He has gone back
    to folding cloth.

--- ENTRY POINT B: DERTHERT MENTIONS FERRAND INDIRECTLY ---

  At mid relationship, if player asks Derthert about his intelligence sources:
    "I have various sources. Some are reliable. Some tell me what I want
    to hear. Some tell me what is true." He looks at the player.
    "The most reliable one tells me things about settlement conditions that
    I could not know otherwise." He stops.
  If player asks who: "Someone who moves between settlements regularly.
    He is not important." He has said too much and knows it.
  The player can now watch for cloth merchants. They will encounter Ferrand.

--- ENTRY POINT C: INVESTIGATING GALEND'S FALL ---

  If player asks Derthert at high relationship how Galend fell:
    "I had information about Vrak's criminal enterprise three months before
    Galend fell." He says this once. "I didn't act in time."
  If player asks what information: "Accurate information. Detailed.
    From a source I trust." He does not say Ferrand's name.
    "The failure was mine. I received the information. I didn't know who
    to trust with it. By the time I decided, Galend was gone."
  The player who then encounters Ferrand in a settlement and the conversation
    reaches Layer 2 or 3 above will understand without being told.

FULL THREAD COMPLETION:
  Player has encountered Ferrand and understood the arrangement.
  Player has confirmed with Derthert (indirectly) that the arrangement exists.
  Player has received the Shroud fragment about the suspicious merchant.
  RESULT: Player now has access to Ferrand as an ongoing intelligence source
    in Vlandian settlements. He appears when Vlandian political events create
    information gaps. He never acknowledges the arrangement. He always talks
    about cloth. He is always accurate.

THREAD 2: CALATILD-INGALTHER CONFLICT
The dynastic claim. The council process. The moment it breaks.
================================================================================

BACKGROUND (player discovers this gradually):
  Ingalther has a dynastic claim on Arromanc lands. He has been pressing
  this claim through Vlandia's council process for years. Calatild has
  been blocking it through Cumbert's procedural objections. Vrak's seizure
  of Jaculan has changed the calculation — a landless baroness is easier
  to disinherit than a governing one. Every day Calatild holds no fief,
  Ingalther's lawyers are working.

DISCOVERY LAYER 1 — FIRST OBSERVATION:
  Player arrives at Pravend's court.
  Two things are visible if the player pays attention:
    1. Calatild is at court. She has been there since Jaculan fell.
       She is doing work — correspondence, meetings, information management.
       She is not a displaced baroness waiting. She is a governing baroness
       whose fief is elsewhere.
    2. A clerk (Cumbert, Bannerwake-invented) is in the hall with a document.
       He is arguing a procedural point with a Cortain representative.
       Politely. Precisely. For approximately the eighteenth time.
  The player who asks Derthert what the clerk is doing:
    "Cumbert represents Calatild's legal case for restoration of Jaculan.
    He has been filing objections to competing claims." A beat.
    "He is effective at objections." The irony in this is the irony of a
    man who has watched this process for a long time and is not certain
    it will hold.

DISCOVERY LAYER 2 — UNDERSTANDING INGALTHER'S POSITION:
  Player reaches mid relationship with Ingalther.
  He states his position directly (he is Honest — confirmed trait):
    "I have a dynastic claim on Arromanc lands that predates Calatild's
    tenure. It is legitimate. I have been pursuing it through proper
    legal channels for several years." He says this as a factual statement.
    "Jaculan is currently held by a criminal under illegitimate authority.
    Calatild holds no fief. The legal framework for her claim is therefore
    weaker than it was."
  He does not say he intends to move on the claim now. He says: "I am
    considering my options." He is always considering his options.

DISCOVERY LAYER 3 — THE COUNCIL MOMENT:
  TRIGGER: Ingalther's legal team files a new challenge to Calatild's
    claim while her status is landless.
  World event fires: "A formal legal challenge to the Arromanc succession
    has been filed with the Vlandian crown. Baron Ingalther dey Cortain
    asserts a dynastic claim predating the current Countess's tenure.
    A council session has been called."

SCENE — THE COUNCIL SESSION:
  Player who is in Pravend or arrives quickly can attend.
  Ingalther's representative presents the claim. Technically correct.
  Cumbert objects. His eighteenth objection. Also technically correct.
  The council is evenly divided. It has been evenly divided before.
  Derthert listens and does not rule immediately.
  Then Calatild speaks.
  She has not spoken publicly about this case before. She has let
  Cumbert manage it. This is the moment the controlled performance ends.

  CALATILD (curt voice, but louder than she speaks in private):
    "Ingalther has a dynastic claim on Arromanc lands. I am aware of this.
    What he has not stated publicly, and what I will state publicly now, is
    that the claim is worth nothing if there is no one capable of governing
    the lands in question. Jaculan was prosperous for fourteen years under
    my governance. I have the records. I have the headmen's testimony.
    I have the harbor schedules and the merchant licenses and the tax rolls.
    Ingalther has a document."
    A beat. Her voice is absolutely controlled.
    "When Ingalther has governed anything for fourteen years and has its
    records available for review, I will reconsider the comparison."
  She sits down.
  Ingalther does not respond immediately. He is recalculating.
  Derthert adjourns the session without ruling.

PLAYER CHOICE AT THE COUNCIL SESSION:
  The player who says nothing: they have witnessed the confrontation.
    The information is available for future use.
  The player who publicly supports Calatild's claim: Calatild notes it.
    Ingalther notes it. Both respond accordingly.
    See their respective HIGH RELATIONSHIP notes above.
  The player who publicly supports Ingalther's claim: Calatild goes cold.
    Ingalther is pleased in a specific, filed way.
    Calatild's relationship drops. Her intelligence network becomes
    unavailable. She begins treating the player as Ingalther's instrument.
  The player who asks Derthert for his view:
    He says, privately, after the session:
    "Ingalther is correct about the legal document. Calatild is correct
    about everything else." He says nothing more. He is going to have
    to rule eventually. He does not know which way is survivable.

THREAD RESOLUTION OPTIONS:
  A. Player restores Jaculan to Calatild (through military action).
     Ingalther's claim loses its leverage. He suspends it — doesn't
     abandon it, suspends it. Council stabilizes. See Calatild HIGH
     RELATIONSHIP for Calatild's response.
  B. Ingalther takes Jaculan through his own claim after Vrak's collapse.
     Calatild becomes a different kind of threat. She begins organizing
     the barons who have been waiting to oppose Ingalther. Derthert has
     the hardest council session of his reign.
  C. Player lets the process run. Cumbert's objections continue failing.
     Eventually Derthert rules. His ruling shapes Vlandian stability for
     the rest of the campaign. It may be possible to influence his ruling
     through the relationship established in Thread 1 (Derthert-Ferrand).
